# Deep Dive — Four Candidates, Scored for Amazon Headway (KDP only, no purchased ISBN)

Research date: September 2026. Candidates: The Stories I Made Up; The Words We Brought With Us;
Lo Que Quiero Que Sepas; Still Me. Sources at the end.

---

## 1. The constraint first: what "no ISBN" costs each book

All four are fill-in books. KDP classified the flagship as low-content at review, and it will do
the same to these. For a low-content title the free KDP ISBN is unavailable, so publishing without
buying one means **"Publish without an ISBN."** KDP's own rules for that option:

| Lost | Effect on these books |
|---|---|
| Look Inside / Read Sample | Buyers can't page through. This matters most for prompt-driven books, where the prompts are the product. Mitigation: the image carousel (seven slots) and A+ Content (seven modules) can show a dozen real pages, and both are available without an ISBN. Every competitor in these niches with an ISBN has Look Inside, so our page photography must be better than their sample. |
| Back-cover thumbnail | The back cover copy only appears in A+ and images. Minor. |
| Series page | No Words to Keep series page. Brand carried by cover consistency, author name, A+ brand module. |
| Expanded Distribution | No bookstore or library wholesale. Immaterial for these four except Still Me, where memory-care facilities would have bought through wholesale; they can still buy on Amazon. |
| Hardcover | Can also be published without an ISBN. Same losses. |

What is not lost: an ASIN, Amazon Ads, A+ Content, Author Central, reviews, categories, keywords,
and the product page itself. The ISBN-free path is workable for every one of the four. The book
that suffers most is the Spanish edition, because its competitors all have Look Inside and its
buyers are comparison shoppers; the book that suffers least is The Stories I Made Up, which sells
on the idea and one photograph.

One exception worth knowing: **Still Me can be built so that it is not low-content.** If the first
half is a real guide (how to do life-story work with someone who is losing memory, what to ask and
when to stop, how care staff use the book, 8,000 to 10,000 words) and the second half is the
fill-in, it is a book with worksheets, not a prompt journal. That is a legitimate design, not a
dodge, and it also raises the price the book can carry. It does not guarantee KDP's reviewer
agrees; it makes the case defensible.

## 2. The scoring model

Five factors, weighted for one question: which of these can make headway on Amazon within a year.

| Factor | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Proven demand | 30% | Do people already search for and buy something like this? |
| Competitive gap | 25% | How weak is what exists? |
| Discoverability without Look Inside | 15% | Are there search terms and ad targets that find the buyer? |
| Build cost and speed | 15% | Weeks to publish, outside costs |
| Brand fit and cross-sell | 15% | Does it sell the flagship, and vice versa? |

## 3. Candidate A — The Stories I Made Up

**Refined concept.** A keepsake for the bedtime stories a parent invents. Front: "The cast," eight
pages where the child draws the recurring characters and the parent writes who they are. Middle: 40
story spreads, each with a title line, "first told" date, a "requested" tally box, a page and a half
of ruled space, and a small unruled box for the kid's drawing. Back: "The one you always asked for"
(four pages), "Stories that never got finished," and a page for the child, years later: "What I
remember about these." 7 x 10, 120 pages.

**Buyer.** A mother buying for the father of a 3-to-8-year-old (Father's Day, Christmas, a new-dad
gift), or a grandparent who tells stories on sleepovers. The parent fills it in over months; no
completion problem because each story is one sitting and the child asks for it.

**Demand evidence.** No product exists. Adjacent demand is real: "Make Your Own Bedtime Story"
(a write-and-draw book for kids, not for parents' stories) sells; personalized bedtime books
(Wonderbly, I See Me) are a large category; Etsy's "bedtime story gift" market page has hundreds of
listings, none of them this. The demand is latent: nobody types "book for the stories I make up."
They type "gifts for dad from kids," "new dad gift," "bedtime story book," "dad journal." Those are
high-volume terms, and the product answers all of them once the buyer sees one photograph.

**Competition.** None direct. The risk is that once it works, KDP clones follow within a season.
The defense is the cast pages, the drawing boxes, and the design, which clones will copy badly.

**Discoverability without Look Inside.** Strong. This book sells on a single image: an open spread
with a dad's handwriting and a five-year-old's drawing of the dragon. It is also the most shareable
object of the four (TikTok and Instagram: "my dad wrote down every story he ever told me"). Ads
target gift terms, not category terms.

**Cost and price.** Print at 7 x 10, 120 pages, cream: about $3.04. List $18.99, royalty about
$8.35. Hardcover about $6.70 print, list $26.99.

**Build.** Ten days. Lightest manuscript of the four (about 1,200 words). Needs a small illustration
system for the cast pages and drawing boxes; no illustration of stories themselves.

**Risks.** Latent demand means the first hundred sales come from ads and social, not search. Dads
are the intended writer and dads are the least likely buyers of a journal; the buyer is the mother,
so the listing must speak to her. Seasonal: Father's Day and Christmas, plus year-round new-baby.

**Scores.** Demand 5 · Gap 10 · Discoverability 7 · Build 9 · Brand fit 7. **Weighted 7.4.**

## 4. Candidate B — The Words We Brought With Us

**Refined concept.** A heritage-language keepsake written by the family member who still has the
language. Sections: "How to say my name, and yours." "The words we still use" (60 entry pages: the
word, how to say it, what it means, when we said it, who said it best). "Things my mother shouted
from the kitchen." "The songs" (lyrics as sung, with a line for the tune's name). "The blessings,
the curses, and the jokes." "Words that don't translate, and what they mean anyway." "Recipes with
their real names." Letters in both languages. Every page has a "say it to me" line for the
grandchild to write the pronunciation the way they hear it. 7 x 10, 130 pages. Language-agnostic:
the writer supplies the language, so one book serves Italian, Polish, Tagalog, Greek, Korean,
Arabic, Yoruba, and Spanish families without translation.

**Buyer.** The second-generation adult (30 to 55) buying for a parent or grandparent, at the moment
they realize the language ends with them. The research is unambiguous: by the third generation the
heritage language is gone in most families, "surviving only in a few phrases saved for
grandparents," and two-thirds of U.S.-born Asian Americans speak only English at home. The buyer
knows this feeling by name.

**Demand evidence.** No product exists on Amazon or Etsy. Demand is latent but targetable, because
ethnic gift searches are real and large: "gifts for Italian grandma," "Filipino grandmother gift,"
"Polish grandma gift," "Korean mom gift," "Greek yiayia gift." One book, dozens of keyword families.
The "language family tree" Etsy market and the heritage-language-loss literature show the emotion is
widely felt and articulated.

**Competition.** None. Adjacent: recipe keepsakes (which the book partly absorbs), and generic
grandparent journals.

**Discoverability without Look Inside.** Medium to strong. The concept explains itself in a title
and one image (an entry page in Italian in an old hand, with a grandchild's pronunciation scrawl
beneath). Ads run on ethnic gift terms and on "immigrant parents gift." A+ modules show the same
page written in four languages, which is the whole pitch in one picture.

**Cost and price.** Print about $3.21. List $19.99, royalty about $8.78. Hardcover $27.99.

**Build.** Two weeks. About 2,500 words of prompts and introductions. No translation cost at all,
which is the strategic point.

**Risks.** Latent demand, like A. The writer must be willing to write in their language, and some
elders write it poorly; the "say it to me" pronunciation lines and permission to write phonetically
are there for that. Some buyers will want a language-specific edition ("The Italian Words We Brought
With Us"); that is a later cover variant, not a new book.

**Scores.** Demand 5 · Gap 10 · Discoverability 6 · Build 9 · Brand fit 9. **Weighted 7.4.**

## 5. Candidate C — Lo Que Quiero Que Sepas

**Refined concept.** The flagship, rewritten for Spanish-speaking families in the United States.
Not a translation: the town you left and the one you came to; the language at home and the one
outside; who stayed behind; compadres and comadres; the sayings that don't translate; the
celebrations asked without assuming; faith asked with respect and still optional; the phone calls
home. Same architecture, giver's page, family questions, letters, index. 7 x 10, 254 pages.

**Buyer.** The bilingual adult child (25 to 50) buying for a Spanish-dominant parent or grandparent.
Día de las Madres on May 10 and U.S. Mother's Day the same week are the largest single window in
these households; Christmas and Día del Padre follow.

**Demand evidence.** Proven. The category exists and has a leader: Mason's "Mamá, quiero escuchar
tu historia" (his listing claims "tens of thousands" bought) and "Abuela, quiero escuchar su
historia"; the "Cuéntame la historia de tu vida" series (mamá, abuela, abuelo); at least five KDP
entries from 2021 to 2025. Amazon says U.S. Hispanics are its fastest-growing Kindle market and
that 30% of the top 100 Spanish bestsellers are self-published, which means self-published Spanish
titles do rank. Only about 4% of U.S. book sales are in Spanish, so the ceiling is lower than the
English flagship's, but the shelf is short and the buyers are concentrated on a date.

**Competition.** Moderate in count, weak in quality. Every title is a translation of an English
prompt set, with the same fifty questions, KDP-grade covers, and 6 x 9 formats. None is adapted to
the family's actual life. None has the giver's page, the family questions, or the letters.

**Discoverability without Look Inside.** Weakest of the four. Its competitors all have Look Inside,
its buyers compare, and Spanish-language search on Amazon.com is thinner and noisier. Mitigation:
Spanish-language A+ with a dozen real pages, and ads on "regalo para mamá," "día de las madres
regalo," "libro para mamá," "abuela regalo." The English flagship's page should cross-link in A+
("También en español").

**Cost and price.** Print $5.32 (254 pages). List $19.99, royalty $6.67. Outside cost: a
native-speaking editor with literary judgment, $800 to $1,500. This is the only candidate with a
real outside cost, and it is the whole product; a translation-grade Spanish book joins the pile it
was meant to beat.

**Build.** Three to four weeks including the editor's pass. The pipeline is unchanged; only the
manuscript file changes.

**Risks.** Editor quality. Regional Spanish (Mexican, Caribbean, Central American, Castilian) is a
choice: recommend neutral Latin American Spanish with a note that the writer should use their own
words. The low-content classification and no-ISBN path apply exactly as they did to the flagship.

**Scores.** Demand 8 · Gap 7 · Discoverability 5 · Build 6 · Brand fit 10. **Weighted 7.4.**

## 6. Candidate D — Still Me

**Refined concept.** Two books in one binding, filled in by the family with the person. Part One,
"This is me," is a life story in large type and short answers: where I'm from, who mattered, the
work, what I'm proud of, the jokes, the songs, written by the person or transcribed by the family
over a few visits. Part Two, "What helps," is for anyone caring for them: what calms me, what
upsets me, the routine, the food, the music, the names to say, what I was like before, what I can
still do, how to help me on a bad day. Photographs on every spread. A removable one-page summary at
the front that care staff can read in two minutes. If built with a real guide section (how to do
life-story work, when to stop, how to use the book in a care setting), it becomes a book with
worksheets rather than a prompt journal. 8.5 x 11, 120 pages.

**Buyer.** The daughter (one in three dementia caregivers is a daughter) in the weeks after
diagnosis, or at the move into memory care. Also activity directors and social workers in memory
care, who buy in fives and tens.

**Demand evidence.** 7.4 million Americans live with Alzheimer's dementia; a diagnosis every 65
seconds; 11 million unpaid family caregivers. Search terms exist and are used: "memory book for
dementia patients," "life story book dementia," "gifts for someone with dementia." Amazon has
several KDP memory books in the niche ("Memory Book for Alzheimer's Patients," "Before I Forget,"
"My Life Story Journal: A Reminiscence Workbook"), which proves buyers exist; the Flapen niche for
dementia products (all product types, 24 tracked) is about $988K a year, which says the Amazon
ceiling is modest. Clinical templates ("This is me," "My Life Story") are used daily in care
settings and are free PDFs, which proves the format works and the institutional buyer exists.

**Competition.** Present and weak. The KDP titles are 6 x 9 prompt lists with stock-photo covers.
The one serious workbook ("The Story of a Life") is a caregiver interview tool, not a keepsake. No
product combines the life story and the caregiver half, and none is something a family would want
to hold.

**Discoverability without Look Inside.** Strong. Search terms exist; the caregiver is an urgent,
motivated buyer; and the institutional channel (memory-care activity directors, Alzheimer's
Association chapters, hospice social workers) works by word of mouth, not by Look Inside. If the
guide section earns a regular-book classification, Look Inside comes back anyway.

**Cost and price.** Print at 8.5 x 11, 120 pages: about $3.04 (large trim rate). List $21.99,
royalty about $10.15. Hardcover $29.99.

**Build.** Two to three weeks. The guide section is real writing (8,000 words) and should be
reviewed by a dementia-care professional; budget $300 to $500 for that read, or find one who will do
it for a credit.

**Risks.** Tone. Every word must be warmer than the flagship and never clinical or pitying. Smaller
ceiling than the Spanish edition. Reviews will be emotional and will mention outcomes we cannot
control. Never claim therapeutic effect; say "used in care settings" and cite the reminiscence
literature once, plainly.

**Scores.** Demand 7 · Gap 8 · Discoverability 8 · Build 7 · Brand fit 8. **Weighted 7.55.**

## 7. Ranking and what it means

| Rank | Book | Score | The one-line case |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Still Me | 7.55 | Real search demand, urgent buyer, weak incumbents, an institutional channel, and the only one that can plausibly avoid the low-content trap |
| 2 (tie) | Lo Que Quiero Que Sepas | 7.4 | Proven category, a date on the calendar, and a leader who is beatable on quality; the editor is the whole risk |
| 2 (tie) | The Words We Brought With Us | 7.4 | The biggest upside of the four and zero translation cost; demand is latent, so it needs ads and social to start |
| 2 (tie) | The Stories I Made Up | 7.4 | Fastest to build, the most giftable and shareable object; latent demand, gift-term ads |

The scores are close because the four are good for different reasons. Read them as: **D and C are
the ones that will sell from search. A and B are the ones that will sell from being seen.** A
portfolio wants both.

## 8. Recommended order, with the calendar doing the deciding

| When | Book | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Now to October 15 | **The Stories I Made Up** | Ten days to build; the only one that can be live for Christmas 2026 with time for reviews; new-dad and Christmas gift terms |
| November to January | **Still Me** | No season; the guide section takes writing time; January is when families act on what they saw at Christmas |
| January to March | **Lo Que Quiero Que Sepas** | Editor's pass in January; live by March 1 for Día de las Madres on May 10 |
| March to April | **The Words We Brought With Us** | Live by mid-April for Mother's Day; pairs with the Spanish edition in ads to immigrant families |

All four use the existing pipeline and the cover system (a different ground color per book; the
same type). All four publish without an ISBN. All four get the seven-image carousel and A+ built
from a printed proof, which is the substitute for Look Inside and is not optional.

## 9. What would change the ranking

- If KDP accepts Still Me as a regular book (free ISBN, Look Inside, series), it moves clearly to first.
- If a strong Spanish editor is found quickly, C moves to first on revenue, because it is the only one with a proven category and a deadline.
- If the first hundred Stories I Made Up buyers post photographs, A's latent demand becomes real demand faster than anything else here, and it becomes the brand's most visible book.
