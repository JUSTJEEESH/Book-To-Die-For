# New Book Ideas — A Ranked Portfolio for Words to Keep

Research date: September 2026. Sources at the end. Every idea here was checked against what exists
on Amazon, what sells, and what buyers complain about. Ideas that failed that check are listed at
the bottom with the reason, so they are not re-proposed later.

---

## 1. What the market says right now

- **Legacy journals are the top of the guided-journal category, not a corner of it.** "Dad, I Want to Hear Your Story" holds the number one rank in Guided Journals with 13,000+ reviews. A KDP-grade "Mom's Life Story Journal" at $24.99 moves about 7,000 copies a month, the only keepsake title in the category's top ten by unit sales.
- **Fill-in books the giver completes are the biggest format in gift books.** Lea Redmond's "Letters to My…" series: 4 million copies, on Oprah's list three times. Knock Knock's "What I Love About You" line is the mass-market version. The buyer fills it in, so there is no completion problem.
- **Two-way journals are a proven category.** Katie Clemons' "Between Mom and Me" series: 500,000 families, 5,500 reviews, praised by the Wall Street Journal.
- **The end-of-life organizer is a mass-market product with a weak incumbent.** "I'm Dead, Now What?" (Peter Pauper, $10) dominates. Its mined complaints are paper quality, value for money, and that "users will outgrow it quickly once information changes." Its contents are identical to the same publisher's "Peace of Mind Planner." KDP clones are numerous and worse.
- **Q&A a Day** (Potter): 2.5 million copies, proving that a multi-year, tiny-daily-entry format works when the object is beautiful.
- **Korie Herold**: 1 million copies across 15 keepsake books, all on materials and design. The baby book "As You Grow" is the anchor.
- **Spanish-language legacy journals exist, but every one is a translation** of an English KDP title (Mason, "Cuéntame la historia de tu vida"). None is designed for a Hispanic family's actual questions.
- **Dementia life-story books** are free clinical PDFs (Alzheimer's Society "This is me," Dementia UK "My Life Story") or one caregiver workbook. Nothing on Amazon is designed as a keepsake a family would want to hold.
- **Dignity Therapy** (Chochinov, 2002) is a validated palliative-care intervention with a published nine-question protocol that produces a "legacy document." There is no consumer book built on it.
- **Birthday-letter books** (a letter to your child every year to 18) exist as KDP-grade paperbacks with gendered florals, and one cloth Compendium book. The format is loved; the objects are weak.
- **Living-eulogy** exists only as a gratitude-practice journal, not a gift book.
- **Two-parent, two-voice books** exist only as a digital product (Meminto). Nothing in print lets both parents answer the same question side by side.

## 2. Selection rules

An idea makes the list only if it passes all four:

1. **Same buyer or same moment** as the flagship: an adult child, a milestone, a gift that says "I want to keep you." The brand is Words to Keep. Everything must be something a family keeps.
2. **Either the giver fills it in (immediate gratification) or completion is designed for.** The two documented failure modes of prompt journals are abandonment and intimidation; each concept must answer them.
3. **Proven demand with a beatable incumbent, or a genuine gap with an obvious buyer.** Not "no one has done this" alone; "no one has done this and here is who buys it."
4. **Buildable on the pipeline.** Manuscript in the tagged format, the interior build, the cover system. Each book is one to three weeks, not a new project.

## 3. Tier one: build next, in this order

### A. WHAT YOU'LL NEED TO KNOW — the practical companion

*The things my family will need to find, know, and do. In one place, in my handwriting.*

**What it is.** The end-of-life organizer, done as a serious, well-made book instead of a $10 joke workbook. People to call. Where every document is. Accounts and what to do about them (with a pointer system, never passwords on the page). The house, the car, the pets, the subscriptions. Medical wishes and the name of the person who decides. What I want done my way, and what I don't care about. A "first 48 hours" checklist for whoever is holding the book. And ten personal pages at the end that hand off to the flagship: the eulogy I'd want, the song, the one thing I want said.

**Why it wins.** Demand is proven by the incumbent. The incumbent's complaints are exactly the things we do well: paper and value (7 x 10, real writing room, a cover that looks like it belongs on a shelf), tone (no jokes on the cover of a book about your mother's death), and "outgrown when information changes," which we solve with dated revision lines on every list page and a "what changed" log at the back, so the book is updated rather than replaced.

**The pairing.** This is the other half of the flagship: the heart and the practical. Same buyer, same moment, same shelf. Listing copy and A+ content cross-sell both. A two-book bundle in the imagery.

**Risks.** KDP will classify it low-content; buy the ISBN up front. Never imply legal validity; the copyright page and the opener say plainly it is not a will. Title check: "What You'll Need to Know" is generic enough to be free.

**Format and price.** 7 x 10, 160–180 pages, ink cover in a second color (charcoal with cream) so the pair reads as a set. $19.99 paperback, $27.99 hardcover. Buildable in two weeks. Christmas 2026 is reachable if it publishes by early November.

### B. READ THIS WHEN — letters for the days I might not be there

*Thirty letters from a parent, written now, for the moments that are coming.*

**What it is.** A book of letters addressed to moments rather than birthdays: read this when you fail at something that mattered; when you get married; when you have your first child; when you're furious with me; when you don't know what to do; when you're forty; when I'm gone. Each letter has a heading, a "written on" date, a short prompt beneath the heading to get the pen moving, and two pages. A contents page at the front lists the moments so the reader can find the right one on the day. A gentle line on each: *You can read this now. I'd wait.*

**Why it wins.** Redmond's "Letters to Open When" proves the concept, but it is a set of envelopes with twelve prompts. KDP birthday-letter books prove the yearly-letter format but look like clip art. A book of moment-letters with real prompts, in the flagship's voice, does not exist. It sells twice: to parents of new babies (write over years) and to parents of graduates and newlyweds (write it in a weekend, give it at the ceremony).

**Completion.** Thirty letters is finite and the moments are vivid; the prompt under each heading does the work the flagship's "quick ones" do. The giver's page and the "if you only write five" list carry over.

**Format and price.** 7 x 10, about 96 pages, deep green cover in the house style. $18.99. Launch for graduation season (May) and Mother's Day 2027.

### C. LO QUE QUIERO QUE SEPAS — the Spanish edition, adapted not translated

**What it is.** The flagship, rewritten for Spanish-speaking families in the United States and Latin America: the town you left and the one you came to; the language spoken at home and the one spoken outside; who stayed behind; the compadres; the sayings that don't translate; how the family celebrates, asked without assuming; faith asked with respect and still optional. The structure, the giver's page, the family-question pages, the letters, and the index all carry over.

**Why it wins.** Every Spanish legacy journal on Amazon is a machine-adjacent translation of an English KDP book. Sixty-five million Hispanic Americans, and Día de las Madres on May 10 is one of the largest gift days of the year in those households. The premium slot is empty.

**Risk.** Translation quality is the whole product. Budget for a native-speaking editor with literary judgment, not a translation service. Every prompt must be read aloud in Spanish by someone whose mother would fill it in.

**Format and price.** Identical to the flagship. $19.99. Target May 2027.

### D. STILL ME — a life-story book for someone living with dementia

*Filled in together, by the family and the person, while it's still easy to ask.*

**What it is.** Two books in one binding. The first half is a life story in the person's own words or the family's transcription: where they came from, who mattered, the work, the jokes, the things they are proud of, written large and simple. The second half is for the people who care for them: what calms them, what upsets them, the routine, the food, the music, the names to say, what they were like before, and what they can still do. Photographs on every spread. Written by the family with the person, at the kitchen table, over a few visits.

**Why it wins.** The clinical versions (Alzheimer's Society "This is me," Dementia UK "My Life Story") prove that care homes and hospitals want exactly this and use it daily. They are PDFs. The one Amazon workbook is a photocopied-looking caregiver tool. No one has made the version a daughter would buy on the day of the diagnosis, and that day comes for about ten million American families a decade. Memory-care staff will recommend it once they have seen one.

**Risk.** Sensitive. The voice must be warmer than the flagship and never clinical. Not a medical document; says so. Smaller market than A or B, but unopposed and evergreen.

**Format and price.** 8.5 x 11 for the caregiver half (large type, photo space), 120 pages. $21.99. Any time; no season.

## 4. Tier two: strong, after tier one has launched

### E. WHAT WE WANT YOU TO KNOW — two voices, one book

The flagship for a couple. Every prompt has two answer areas on the spread, *one of us* and *the other of us*, so a mother and father, or two grandparents, answer the same question in their own hands, side by side. A few prompts are only for the two of them: what we argued about, what we never told the kids, what the other one is wrong about. Nothing like it exists in print; Meminto sells it as a digital product. Gift for a 40th or 50th anniversary, a retirement, a couple's milestone birthday. 7 x 10, 254 pages, $22.99.

### F. WHILE YOU'RE HERE — everything I never said to you

The mirror of the flagship: the adult child writes to the parent and gives the finished book. Forty prompts in the flagship's voice: the thing you did that I never thanked you for; the day I understood you; what I'd tell my kids about you; what I'm sorry for; what I'll keep. Competes with Redmond ("Letters to My Mom," 4 million) and Knock Knock ("What I Love About Mom"), both light. Ours is adult and serious. No completion problem: the buyer is the writer. Mother's Day and Father's Day. 6 x 9, 96 pages, $16.99. Crowded, but the demand is enormous and the top of the market is empty.

### G. UNTIL YOU'RE EIGHTEEN — a letter for every birthday

Bought at birth, given at eighteen. One spread per year: a letter, and a one-page snapshot (what you loved, what you said, what you were afraid of, what the world was doing). Gender-neutral, typographic, in the house style. The baby-shower gift market is huge and the incumbents in this exact format are weak. Competes at the edge of Korie Herold's baby-book territory, which is a materials fight we lose, so this one is positioned as letters, not a baby book. 7 x 10, 120 pages, $22.99.

### H. SAY IT NOW — for anyone with a serious illness, and the people helping them

Built on the nine questions of Dignity Therapy, expanded gently, with a section for the person helping to write (most will be too tired to hold a pen). Evidence-based positioning without clinical claims: "inspired by the questions palliative-care clinicians use." Channel: hospice chaplains and social workers buy in bulk; families buy one. Smaller and slower than A through D, but nothing like it exists for consumers, and it is the most defensible book on this list. 7 x 10, 80 pages, large type, $16.99.

## 5. Tier three: keep on the list

- **Faith edition of the flagship.** Thomas Nelson's "A Grandparent's Legacy" (2007) owns the Christian slot with a dated twelve-month format. A faith edition with scripture and prayer prompts, same interior otherwise, would sell in Christian bookstores through expanded distribution. Only after the flagship's own classification question is settled.
- **Waiting for You.** An expecting-grandparent journal for the months before the birth. Growing niche, weak incumbents, small.
- **Ask Me Anything.** A book where every question is written by the family, and the parent answers. The family-question page as a whole book. Fun at reunions; untested.
- **Role editions** of the flagship (Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa). Already planned; only after 100 reviews.

## 6. Checked and rejected

- **Pet memory books.** Crowded, low price ceiling, incumbents are fine.
- **Teacher or coach memory books.** Seasonal, low ticket, no brand fit.
- **Shadow-work, manifestation, gratitude, five-minute formats.** Huge, trend-driven, and not what the brand means. Words to Keep is about other people.
- **Daily or weekly formats** (a question a day for a year). Schedules get missed; the flagship's research showed why.
- **Anything with envelopes, pockets, or seals.** KDP can't.
- **A digital or QR-based voice product.** KDP can't host it and the value is in the hand.

## 7. Sequence and economics

| Order | Book | Build time | Launch window | Why then |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A. What You'll Need to Know | 2 weeks | Publish by Nov 1, 2026 | Christmas; pairs with the flagship's first season |
| 2 | B. Read This When | 2 weeks | March 2027 | Graduation and Mother's Day |
| 3 | C. Lo Que Quiero Que Sepas | 3–4 weeks (editor) | April 2027 | Día de las Madres, May 10 |
| 4 | D. Still Me | 2 weeks | Any | Evergreen |
| 5 | E, F, G, H | 2 weeks each | Staggered | After the first four have reviews |

Every book shares the pipeline, the type system, and the cover system (ink and cream for the legacy line; a second color per book so a shelf of them reads as a family). Every listing cross-sells the others. The series page is unavailable to low-content titles, so the brand has to be carried by cover consistency, the author name, and A+ content instead.

## 8. The ones I would bet on

If only two get built: **A** and **B**. A has the largest proven demand and the weakest incumbent of anything on this list. B is the most emotionally powerful object and sells at two life moments a year. C is the biggest untouched market but depends on finding the right editor.
