# Never Done Before — Ideas Checked Against Amazon

Research date: September 2026. Forty-eight concepts were generated across categories, then each was
checked against Amazon and the wider web. Verdicts use three words:

- **OPEN**: nothing on Amazon does this. The first good version defines the category.
- **WEAK**: something exists, but it is a blank logbook, a clip-art KDP paperback, a PDF, or a
  single old title. A well-made version wins on sight.
- **TAKEN**: a real product exists and is fine. Not worth a fight unless noted.

The top of this file is the shortlist. The full ledger follows. Sources are at the end.

---

## The shortlist: open or nearly open, with a real buyer

Ranked by how much a buyer wants it multiplied by how empty the shelf is.

### 1. The Stories I Made Up — OPEN
*The bedtime stories a parent invents, written down before they're forgotten.*
Every parent who improvises bedtime stories has a recurring cast (the dragon who was afraid of
stairs, the truck that wanted to be a boat) that vanishes when the child turns eight. Nothing on
Amazon captures them. Structure: a cast page (characters, drawn by the kid), then one spread per
story with a "first told" date and "requested ___ times" tally, and a "the one you always asked
for" section. The kid illustrates. Buyer: parents of 3-to-8-year-olds; a father's gift in
particular. Immediate use, no completion problem, and it becomes the object the adult child keeps.
7 x 10, 120 pages, $18.99.

### 2. The Words We Brought With Us — OPEN
*A heritage-language keepsake: the words, sayings, and songs of the family's first language, written by the person who still has it.*
A grandmother writes the Italian, Polish, Tagalog, Yoruba, Spanish, or Greek words the family
still uses, how to say them, what they mean, when she'd say them, and the ones that don't translate.
The lullaby. The thing her mother shouted from the kitchen. Nothing on Amazon does this. Buyer: the
grandchild or adult child of an immigrant, at exactly the moment they realize the language stops
with Grandma. Structure: word pages (word, how to say it, what it means, when we said it, who said
it best), a songs section, a "how to swear" page (humor), letters in both languages. Works for any
language because the writer supplies it. 7 x 10, 130 pages, $19.99.

### 3. What You'll Need to Know at Work — OPEN
*The handover book. Everything a retiring or departing person knows that isn't written anywhere.*
Corporate knowledge-transfer is a consulting industry; the personal, physical version does not
exist. Written by the person leaving, for the person arriving: who to call and what they're
actually like, the things that break and how to fix them, the unwritten rules, the customer who
needs handling, the mistake I made in year one, what I'd change if I were you. A retirement gift a
team buys for a colleague, or a book a company buys by the box. Also a manager's tool. 7 x 10,
120 pages, $19.99. Sells year-round; retirement season peaks in June and December.

### 4. Both Sides — OPEN as a format
*A flip book. One side is the parent's. Turn it over and the other side is the child's. They meet in the middle.*
The tête-bêche binding (two front covers, contents meeting head-to-tail) is a real print format
that KDP can produce with no special handling: the second half of the interior is rotated 180
degrees. No two-way journal on Amazon uses it. It solves the problem every shared journal has:
whose book is it? Here it is both, physically. Prompts mirror each other: the parent's page "what I
was afraid of when you were born" faces, on the flip side, the child's "what I think you were
afraid of." Buyer: parents of teens and adult children. 6 x 9, 200 pages, $19.99.

### 5. How We Came Here — OPEN
*The immigration story, written by the person who made the trip, for the ones born after.*
Amazon has picture books about immigration and memoir-writing guides. It has no keepsake for an
immigrant parent or grandparent to write the story: the decision, what was packed, the crossing,
the first apartment, the first job, the word they mispronounced for a year, what they miss, what
they'd do again. Works for any origin because the writer supplies it. Pairs with #2. Buyer: the
adult child, often around a citizenship ceremony, a parent's 70th, or a death in the family back
home. 7 x 10, 140 pages, $19.99.

### 6. What Runs in Us — OPEN
*A family's health story, told as a story, for the children who'll need it.*
Every doctor asks "does anything run in your family?" and nobody knows. The only products are
medical logbooks. This is the narrative version: who had what, at what age, what it was like, what
they wish they'd caught earlier, the scars and what they're from, the surgeries, the mental health
nobody discussed, the things that skipped a generation, plus a clean one-page summary a child can
photograph and show a physician. Buyer: adults 40 to 70, for their kids; also a companion to the
practical book already planned. 7 x 10, 100 pages, $17.99.

### 7. The Book of Bets — WEAK
*A household ledger of bets, predictions, and dares, with a settlement page and a ten-year open date.*
Kids' time-capsule activity books exist. Nothing serves the adult version: family and friend-group
bets ("he'll never move out of that apartment"), predictions written on New Year's Eve, dares,
stakes, and a page to settle up years later. Gift for couples, roommates, siblings, bar-and-cabin
households. Funny on the shelf, serious in ten years. 6 x 9, 120 pages, $14.99.

### 8. Our Parents, Together — OPEN
*A workbook for adult siblings facing their parents' aging: the conversation nobody wants to start, with a structure that gets it started.*
Guides and PDFs exist from senior-living companies; there is no fill-in book siblings share. Who
does what, who lives closest, money, the house, the medical decisions, what Mom actually said she
wanted, what each sibling is afraid of, and an agreements page everyone signs. Buyer: the sibling
who has been carrying it alone. Small, serious, and the only one. 8.5 x 11, 90 pages, $18.99.

### 9. The Name We Gave You — OPEN
*A whole book about a child's name.*
Baby name lists and personalized story books exist; a keepsake about the story of one name does
not: the names we almost chose, who you're named after and their story, what it means, how each
grandparent reacted, the nicknames that came and went, what strangers get wrong, and letters from
the namesakes. Small, cheap to make, universal, a shower gift that isn't another baby book.
7 x 7, 60 pages, $14.99.

### 10. Every Place I've Lived — OPEN
*A life told through addresses, with room to draw each one.*
Reminiscence therapists use "draw the house you grew up in" because it unlocks memory better than
any question. No book does it. One spread per address: draw the floor plan or the front, then who
lived there, what you could hear at night, what happened there, why you left. Buyer: adults 60+,
and their children; also memory-care activity staff. 8.5 x 11 landscape for drawing, 100 pages,
$19.99.

### 11. What We Said — OPEN
*The book that collects the eulogies, toasts, and letters after a funeral, so the family keeps what was said.*
Funeral guest books collect names. Nothing collects the words: the eulogies as delivered, the
stories told at the reception, the letters that came in the weeks after, in the hands of the
people who wrote them. Buyer: the family member organizing the service; funeral homes as a bulk
buyer. 8.5 x 11, 100 pages with room to paste printed eulogies, $19.99.

### 12. Who You Were to Us — WEAK
*The grandparent you never met, written for a child by the parent who lost them.*
One picture book with activity pages exists. The keepsake written by a parent (what she'd have
called you, what she'd have cooked for you, what she said when I told her I was pregnant) does
not. Buyer: any parent whose parent died before the grandchild could know them. Strong emotion,
small page count, deep shelf life. 7 x 7, 72 pages, $16.99.

## What the pattern says

The empty shelves cluster in three places, and they say something about how to think about new
books:

1. **Things only the writer knows that vanish with them:** invented stories, a language, an
   immigration, a health history, what was said at a funeral. These are all Words to Keep books.
   The brand can carry every one of them.
2. **Moments with a buyer but no object:** naturalization, the first-generation graduate, the
   siblings' conversation, a retirement handover. Each has a person with a credit card and a
   feeling, and nothing to buy.
3. **Formats nobody has used:** the flip book, the draw-it book, the bets ledger. Format novelty is
   rare on KDP because most KDP publishers are not designers. It is our advantage.

The shelves that looked open and weren't: obituaries, tattoos, cars, songs, heirlooms, godparents,
deployments, widows, stepfamilies. Someone already made a decent book for each. The lesson is that
"nobody has done this" is usually wrong for anything that can be named in three words; the open
ground is in the ideas that take a sentence to explain.

## Recommended next three, if building from this list

1. **The Stories I Made Up** (#1): fastest to build, most joyful, a gift that gets photographed.
2. **What You'll Need to Know at Work** (#3): a new buyer (teams, companies) the current brand does not reach, and bulk orders.
3. **The Words We Brought With Us** (#2) paired with **How We Came Here** (#5): two books, one audience, one launch, and the Spanish edition of the flagship already planned for the same families.
