# Cover directions: what the research says, and six prompts to try

**Book:** *What I Want You to Know* (flagship, 7 x 10, paperback and hardcover). Companion to `COVER-V2-PROMPT-AND-PLACEMENT.md`, which holds the text placement, fonts, and wrap geometry. Those do not change with the art direction; only the ground color and the type color do (noted per direction).

## 1. What the category's covers look like right now

| Book | Cover | Why it works | Rank signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Grandma's Story* / *Mom's Story*, Korie Herold (Paige Tate / Blue Star) | Linen hardcover, gold foil title, small botanical device, lots of empty cloth | Reads as a keepsake object, not a book. Premium at arm's length and on a gift table. | #1 in Grandparenting on the snapshot found; $24.95; 112 pp |
| *Mom / Dad / Grandmother, I Want to Hear Your Story*, Jeffrey Mason (Sourcebooks) | Soft solid ground, simple watercolor leaf or floral, large friendly serif | Owns the "I want to hear your story" phrase; the floral says "gift for her", the Dad editions go darker and plainer | 500,000+ copies across the series; 2025 redesigned editions |
| *Tell Me Your Life Story, Grandma / Mom* (Questions About Me) | Pastel watercolor florals, script title | Blends into the gift aisle, which is the point for a $12 book | Top 10 Grandparenting |
| Duncan & Stone *Legacy Journal* | Charcoal or blush linen, minimal foil type, no image | Same keepsake cue as Herold | Toys & Games listing, 85 pp |
| New Seasons *Grandma, Tell Me Your Story* | Photographic hummingbird or hydrangea | Older buyer, older look | #677 Grandparenting |
| *Letters to My Grandchild*, Lea Redmond (Chronicle) | The cover is an envelope with a stamp | One idea, instantly understood | Long-running bestseller, restyled 2024 |

The wall a buyer scrolls is pastel, floral, cream, and script. Two covers in the top ten break the pattern: Herold's linen and the Chronicle envelope, and both are built on **one object a buyer understands in a quarter second.**

## 2. What cover designers are saying about 2026

- **Type carries the cover.** Titles now fill 70 to 80 percent of many covers; "clarity beats cleverness" at phone size (Damonza).
- **Contrast over subtlety.** Pale, low-contrast covers struggle in thumbnails; even nonfiction is moving to strong color and heavy type (Greenleaf, Damonza).
- **Hand-made over polished.** Oil paint, charcoal, cut paper, visible texture. Buyers are learning to read glossy AI render as cheap. Figures used symbolically, not literally (Greenleaf).
- **Oversized handwritten type** is the year's personal-touch trend (Greenleaf).
- **Plain white or cream grounds** are the counter-move on result pages full of dark thumbnails (Damonza).
- **Built for two sizes.** Big shapes and contrast win the click; texture and detail reward the hand. Gift books live or die on the second one too.

## 3. What that means for this book

The current cover fails the first rule (a thin 44 pt title in a 160 px thumbnail). The kitchen-table scene fixed that but reads as "cozy desk", a stock mood rather than *this* book. The directions below each hold to four tests:

1. **One object, understood in a glance,** that means *stories, memories, words kept for family.*
2. **No person, no face, no hands.** The book is for Mom, Dad, Grandma and Grandpa; the art cannot pick one.
3. **Top 40 percent quiet** so a heavy Playfair title owns it.
4. **Hand-made surface:** paint, paper, linen, ink. No glossy render.

Pick with the same seven-point checklist as before (dark or plain top, reads at 160 px, no readable text, no hands, count the objects, warm light, fadeable edge).

## 4. The six directions and their prompts

All prompts: generate **portrait, 2:3**, 4 to 6 variations each. Each prompt ends with the same no-list; keep it, it is doing real work.

### A. The Letter (recommended first)

**Idea.** A cream envelope with a dark wax seal on navy linen. The book *is* a letter; it ends with one. Chronicle's envelope cover has sold for a decade on this exact idea, and nobody in the category does it on a dark ground.
**Type:** cream `#efe7d6` on the navy. Ground for spine and back: `#1e2838`.
**Risk:** a sealed envelope can read as "to be opened after I'm gone". Keep the seal small and the light warm, and keep the pen in frame: it says *being written*, not *left behind*.

```
A painterly book cover illustration, portrait orientation, no text anywhere.

A single cream-colored envelope, slightly worn at the edges, lies on deep navy linen cloth, placed in the lower half of the frame and angled very slightly. The envelope is closed with a small round wax seal in deep navy, almost black. Beside the envelope rests a fountain pen with a warm brass nib. Tucked half out of the envelope is the corner of a folded letter, its lines suggested by soft pen strokes only, no readable words. Soft warm light comes from the upper left, giving the envelope a gentle shadow on the linen.

The upper half of the image is plain deep navy linen with a visible woven texture and nothing else, kept quiet for a title. The envelope is the only bright object and reads clearly even when the image is very small.

Style: modern editorial oil painting on canvas with soft visible brushwork and a tactile, hand-made feel, restrained and premium, like a literary hardcover. Palette: deep navy (#1e2838), cream paper, warm brass, a touch of amber in the light. The image fades to solid deep navy at the left and top edges.

No people, no hands, no faces, no text, no letters, no numbers, no postage stamps, no logos, no borders, no frame, no vignette edge, no florals, no hearts, no candles, no clutter.
```

Variant to try once: replace the wax seal with "tied once around with a thin cream cotton string". Less formal, more Dad.

### B. Linen and gold, one line drawing (the category leader's look, in our color)

**Idea.** Exactly what the #1 book does (linen, foil, small device), printed rather than real. Buyers already read linen plus gold as "heirloom". Our move is the deep navy ground and a device that fits every parent: a house with one lit window. The first prompt in the book is the house they grew up in.
**Type:** set the title in Illustrator in gold, flat `#d4b572` or a gradient `#c9a65a` to `#eadcaa`, Playfair Display Bold. Tagline and author in cream.
**Risk:** at 160 px a dark cover with small gold type is the current problem in a new color. The device must be large (a third of the cover) and the title must be the heavy weight.

```
A book cover background and emblem, portrait orientation, no text anywhere.

The entire image is deep navy linen bookcloth, photographed flat and evenly lit, with a fine visible woven texture and slight natural variation, like the cover of a premium keepsake journal. In the lower-middle of the frame, centered, is a single emblem rendered as if stamped in warm gold foil: a small two-storey house drawn in confident single-weight line, one window lit (a filled gold pane), a chimney, a low fence, and a single bare tree beside it. The emblem is about one third of the image width, clean, with the slight sheen and tiny imperfections of real foil pressed into cloth.

The upper 55 percent of the image is plain navy linen with nothing on it, kept for a title. No other objects.

Style: product photography of a real cloth-bound book cover, tactile, restrained, premium. Palette: deep navy (#1e2838) and warm metallic gold only.

No people, no hands, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no borders, no frame, no florals, no hearts, no extra objects, no glossy highlights, no vignette.
```

Swap the device to taste: "an oak tree with visible roots" (classic, slightly expected), "a key on a loop of ribbon" (what you leave someone), "a pair of reading glasses folded on a closed book" (quietest).

### C. Their handwriting, oversized (the 2026 trend, and the product's whole point)

**Idea.** Cream paper, nothing on it but the title in big handwriting. The book is about their handwriting; the cover shows it. On a results page of dark and pastel thumbnails, a plain cream cover with one bold ink line is the odd one out, which is the job.
**Type:** this one the model does not set. Generate only the paper. In Illustrator set the title in a handwriting face at 90 to 110 pt in navy ink (`#1e2838`), the tagline and author in Playfair/EB Garamond, also navy. Free handwriting faces that look like a real adult hand: Homemade Apple, Nothing You Could Do, La Belle Aurore (all on Google Fonts). Better still, hand-letter it yourself with a brush pen, scan at 600 dpi, and live-trace. A real hand beats every font.
**Risk:** lowest "wow" of the six; wins on clarity, not drama. The spine and back are navy with cream type as before, so the object still looks premium in the hand.

```
A book cover background, portrait orientation, no text anywhere.

The entire image is a sheet of warm cream laid writing paper, photographed flat from directly above in soft daylight, filling the frame edge to edge. The paper shows a gentle fibre texture and faint laid lines, with very subtle natural tone variation and the softest shadow toward the bottom edge. In the lower right corner, small, a single fountain pen lies at a slight angle, its brass nib catching the light, with one tiny dot of navy ink beside the nib. Nothing else.

Style: tactile, hand-made, premium stationery photography, soft and quiet. Palette: warm cream (#efe7d6 to #e6dcc6), deep navy ink (#1e2838), warm brass.

No people, no hands, no text, no letters, no numbers, no writing, no lines, no logos, no borders, no frame, no florals, no clutter, no harsh shadows.
```

If you want the model to try the lettering itself: add "Across the upper two thirds, written large in confident dark navy fountain-pen handwriting with natural pressure variation, the words: What I want you to know" and reject every output where a single letter is wrong. Expect to reject most.

### D. The house at blue hour (your style, warmed up)

**Idea.** A modest house with one lit window, a path, a bare tree, at the moment the sky goes deep blue. It is the first question in the book and the most universal image of "where I come from". It is also the visual language of your menopause cover, so the author brand starts to have a look. Painted, not rendered, so it stays warm.
**Type:** cream on navy sky.
**Risk:** two houses across your two books; a night scene can read lonely. Keep the window warm and the sky blue, not black, and put smoke in the chimney.

```
A painterly book cover illustration, portrait orientation, no text anywhere.

A modest two-storey family house seen from the front at blue hour, just after sunset, set in the lower half of the frame. One downstairs window glows warm amber; a thin curl of smoke rises from the chimney. A short path leads from the bottom edge of the frame to the front door. A single large bare tree stands to one side with a rope swing hanging still. A low wooden fence, a mailbox at the path's end. Light snow or dry autumn leaves on the ground, your choice, kept quiet.

The upper half of the image is a deep, clear navy twilight sky with one or two faint stars, kept almost empty for a title. The lit window is the brightest point in the picture and reads even when the image is very small.

Style: modern editorial oil painting with soft visible brushwork, warm and nostalgic rather than dramatic, like the jacket of a literary novel. Palette: deep navy (#1e2838), warm amber light, cream, muted greens and browns. The image fades to solid deep navy at the top edge.

No people, no hands, no faces, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no borders, no frame, no lightning, no storm, no moon, no cars, no florals, no clutter.
```

### E. The open book, cut from paper (hand-made and symbolic)

**Idea.** An open book from which a small paper-cut world rises: a house, a tree, a road, two birds. The stories inside, made visible. Cut paper is the hand-made look designers are reaching for this year, it cannot be mistaken for a photo, and it is the only direction here that nobody in the category is doing.
**Type:** cream on navy.
**Risk:** busiest of the six; must be simplified hard. If an output has more than five cut shapes, discard. Can tip toward a children's book if the shapes get cute; keep them plain.

```
A paper-cut book cover illustration, portrait orientation, no text anywhere.

An open book, seen slightly from above, lies in the lower third of the frame, its cream pages fanned. Rising from the open pages, as layered cut-paper silhouettes, are a small house with a chimney, a single tree, a winding road, and two small birds in flight, all cut from cream and warm amber paper with soft real shadows between the layers. At most five shapes. The composition is simple and calm, with plenty of empty space.

The upper 55 percent of the image is a flat deep navy paper background with nothing on it, kept for a title.

Style: real hand-cut layered paper art photographed with soft directional light, tactile and clearly made by hand, restrained, premium. Palette: deep navy (#1e2838), cream, warm amber. No gradients except the paper shadows.

No people, no hands, no faces, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no borders, no frame, no hearts, no florals, no glitter, no extra objects, no 3D render look.
```

### F. Old photographs, no faces (memories, literally)

**Idea.** Three or four old photographs with white borders, fanned on dark wood beside a pen, the photographs showing places rather than people: a porch, an old car, a beach, a kitchen. "My memories" in one glance. The nostalgia buyer (and the New Seasons buyer) responds to this immediately.
**Type:** cream on navy.
**Risk:** dated if the photos go sepia and busy; AI likes to put faces in photos, which both breaks the no-person rule and looks fake. The prompt forbids faces; discard any output with one.

```
A painterly book cover illustration, portrait orientation, no text anywhere.

On a dark walnut tabletop, in the lower half of the frame, three old photographs with white borders lie loosely fanned, slightly faded. They show only places and things, never people: a front porch with a rocking chair, an old pickup truck on a dirt road, a kitchen window with a pie cooling. One photograph has a tiny handwritten caption suggested by a short pen stroke, not readable. A fountain pen rests across the corner of the nearest photograph. Soft warm light from the upper left.

The upper half of the image is deep navy, nearly empty, with the warm light fading up into it, kept for a title. The photographs are the brightest objects and read clearly even when the image is very small.

Style: modern editorial oil painting with soft visible brushwork, tactile and hand-made, restrained and premium. Palette: deep navy (#1e2838), cream, faded warm photographic tones, dark walnut. The image fades to solid deep navy at the top and left edges.

No people, no hands, no faces, no figures in the photographs, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no borders, no frame, no florals, no hearts, no sepia filter over the whole image, no clutter.
```

## 5. How I would run the test

1. Generate A, B and D first (six variations each). They are the three strongest on the "one object, one glance" test and the three most different from each other.
2. Build the front only for the best of each, with the title in place, and shrink all three to 160 px beside `build/front-thumb-160.png` and a screenshot of the Amazon results page for "memory journal for mom". The one you can still read and still name the object on wins.
3. Then build the wrap for the winner only.
4. If you cannot choose between two, A beats B when the gold type does not read at 160 px, and D beats A when A's envelope looks like a sympathy card. Those are the two failure modes to watch.

C is the one to keep in reserve: cheapest to make, hardest to get wrong, and if the category keeps going darker it becomes the stand-out.
