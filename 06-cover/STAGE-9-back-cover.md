# STAGE 9 — Back Cover Copy

**Status:** Final. The text is in `back-cover-copy.txt` and set on the wrap.

## The copy

> **Some things are too important to leave unsaid.**
>
> You know some of the stories. You know the person, or the version they've shown you. This book is for the rest.
>
> What I Want You to Know is a guided legacy journal for a parent or grandparent to fill in by hand, for the people who will keep it. It starts with the house they grew up in and ends with a letter. In between are the questions nobody thinks to ask: what they were afraid of, what they changed their mind about, what they never said, what they hope you'll forgive, and what to do on the days you miss them.
>
> Two hundred prompts, one to a page, with room to actually write. Pages where the family adds questions of their own. And a section near the end where the writer stops answering and speaks directly to you.
>
> Their memories. Their wisdom. Their handwriting. Kept, for the people who will want it most.
>
> **Write it while you're here. Keep it forever.**

## What it does, line by line

| Line | Job |
|---|---|
| Some things are too important to leave unsaid. | The emotional premise from the brief, as the headline. |
| You know some of the stories... This book is for the rest. | Names the buyer's real motive in two sentences. |
| What I Want You to Know is a guided legacy journal... | The category words a buyer scans for, stated once, plainly. |
| It starts with the house... ends with a letter. | The arc, in one sentence. Signals this is not a questionnaire. |
| the questions nobody thinks to ask: ... | The differentiator, with five concrete examples that no competitor's back cover could list. |
| Two hundred prompts, one to a page... | The three complaints about the category answered: count, space, and the family's own questions. |
| a section near the end where the writer stops answering and speaks directly to you | The centerpiece, described without naming it. |
| Their memories. Their wisdom. Their handwriting. Kept... | The brief's required beats: memories, wisdom, handwriting, future generations. |
| Write it while you're here. Keep it forever. | The emotional promise, as the last line. Works for the buyer and for the writer. |

Word count: 178. Fits the back panel at 12.6 pt with the headline at 25 pt and generous margins.

Death is not mentioned. "Miss them" and "keep it forever" carry the future without a clock.
