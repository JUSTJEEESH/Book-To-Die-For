If you're giving this book

Four things that make the difference between a book that fills up and one that sits by the bed.

  1. Fill in the next page before you wrap it. Name the storyteller and the listener. Write where the stories usually happen.
  2. Give it with a pen clipped to it. The first story should be written the same night.
  3. Ask the listener to draw the cast first. Once the dragon has a face, the stories come.
  4. Don't ask when it'll be full. Ask which one they told last night.

These are yours.

Somewhere in your house there is a dragon nobody else has ever heard of. A truck that talks. A girl who is also, for reasons that made sense at the time, a sandwich. You made them up at nine o'clock at night with one hand on a small back, and you have told them fifty times, and you could not repeat one of them now if you tried.

That's what this book is for. Not the stories in other books. Yours.

You don't have to write them well. Write them the way you tell them, with the part you always forget and the ending that changes. Write the ones that are two sentences long. Write the one that only works if you do the voice, and then write what the voice was.

Someday the person you told them to will be taller than you, and will want to know whether the dragon was ever really afraid of the stairs. This is where they'll find out.

7

How to use this book

One story to a spread. Give it a title, even if the title is The Truck One.

Write the date you first told it, if you remember. If you don't, write the year and guess.

Tick a box every time it's asked for. The one with the most ticks goes in the back of the book, the long way.

Let the listener draw. There's a box on every story for that, and eight pages at the front for the cast.

Write in pen, in pencil, in crayon. Write in the dark if you have to.

Abandoned stories count. So do stories that were mostly a list of animals.

8

The rules

There aren't any. But since you asked:

Bad stories count.

Stories you borrowed from a movie and then made better count.

If the listener remembers it differently, the listener is right.

You are allowed to write the ending you never got to.
