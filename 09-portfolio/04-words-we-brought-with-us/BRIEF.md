# THE WORDS WE BROUGHT WITH US — Brief (Stages 1 and 2)

**Title:** The Words We Brought With Us
**Tagline:** The words, the sayings, and the songs we still say.
**Series:** Words to Keep · **Author:** Joshua Caleb Green
**Format:** 7 x 10 paperback, cream, matte, about 120 pages, no bleed. Hardcover same interior.
**Price:** $19.99 paperback, $27.99 hardcover.
**KDP:** publish without ISBN (low-content expected).
**Launch:** live by mid-April 2027 for Mother's Day; pairs with the Spanish edition in ads to immigrant families.

## Positioning

A heritage-language keepsake, written by the family member who still has the language, for the
ones who are losing it. The words the family still uses. How to say them. What they mean. When they
were said, and who said them best. The things a mother shouted from the kitchen. The songs, with the
words as they were actually sung. The food by its real name. Letters in both languages.

Language-agnostic by design: the writer supplies the language, so one book serves Italian, Polish,
Tagalog, Greek, Korean, Arabic, Yoruba, Vietnamese, Spanish, and every other family. No translation
cost. Every label is in English; every answer is in theirs.

## Buyer and writer

- **Buyer:** the second-generation adult, 30 to 55, buying for a parent or grandparent at the moment
  they realize the language ends with them. Mother's Day, Christmas, a grandparent's birthday.
- **Writer:** the grandparent or parent who still speaks it. Also the grandchild, on the "how it
  sounds to me" lines, which makes it a book two generations write in.
- **Completion:** entries are short. Two words a visit fills the book in a year.

## Differentiation

Nothing on Amazon or Etsy does this. The research is unambiguous: by the third generation the
heritage language is usually gone, "surviving only in a few phrases saved for grandparents." This
book is those phrases, kept.

## Structure (about 116 pages)

**Front (10):** half title · blank · title · copyright · giver's note · giver's page · opening letter
(*The words are yours*) · how to use · the rules · blank
**Part One — How to say my name (8):** my name · your names, in my language · where the language comes
from · who spoke it to me · quick ones · family question
**Part Two — The words we still use (30):** opener · 26 word pages, two entries each (52 words) · quick
ones · family question
**Part Three — Things my mother shouted from the kitchen (18):** opener · fourteen prompts (what she
called us in trouble, what she said at the door, when we were sick, about money, blessings, the mild
curses, the jokes that only work in our language, the words with no English, how we say I love you) ·
finish these · family question
**Part Four — The songs (14):** opener · ten song pages (the words as we sang them) · the one everyone
knows · family question
**Part Five — The food, by its real name (8):** opener · dishes and what we called them · the recipe (2)
· what we said before eating, and when it was good · quick ones
**Part Six — Letters, in both languages (14):** opener · a letter to you, in my language (2) · the same
letter in English, or as close as I can get (2) · a letter for you to write back (2) · the words I want
you to keep · one last thing to say (2)
**Closing (4):** for the one reading this · anything else · index

## Voice

Warm, dry, quick. The book knows what it is to be the last one who says a word. It never says
"heritage" or "culture" on a page the writer sees. It says "the words," "our language," "at home."
