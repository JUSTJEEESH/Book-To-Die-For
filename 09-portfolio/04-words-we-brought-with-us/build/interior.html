If you're giving this book

Four things that make the difference between a book that fills up and one that doesn't.

  1. Fill in the next page before you wrap it. Say which language, and why you want it kept.
  2. At the end of most parts there's a page called A question from your family. Write on each one a word you half-remember and want back.
  3. Ask them, out loud, the first time you're together: "What did your mother call you when you were in trouble?" Write the answer on the spot, in the book.
  4. Read it back to them, badly. Being corrected is half the point.

If you bought this book for yourself:

This book belongs to

The words are yours.

Someone gave you this book because there are words only you still say. The thing your mother shouted from the kitchen. The name for the soft part of the bread. The song you sang without knowing you were singing. The word that means "I'm tired, but happy, and a little sad, and it's evening," that has no English.

They've heard you say these things their whole lives. They can't say them back. In a generation, nobody will.

This book is for those words. Not the language, all of it. Just the part that lived in your house.

Write them the way you say them. Spell them however you like, in whichever alphabet you like. Put the meaning underneath, and when you said it, and who said it best. There's a line on every entry for the person learning it to write how it sounds to them. Let them. Correct them. That's the whole exercise.

Start with your name.

7

How to use this book

Any language. Any alphabet. Any spelling, as long as it's how you say it.

Two words to a page, in Part Two. Fifty-two in all. If you have more, there's an index at the back with room to keep going.

Every entry has a line for the person learning the word to write how it sounds to them. It will be wrong. Leave it. Write the right way underneath.

Where the book says mother, father, or home, it means whoever and wherever that was for you.

Write in pen. Cross things out. The mistakes are part of the record.

If writing is hard, say the word and let someone write it. Or record it on a phone and write on the page where the recording is kept. The sound of you saying it is the thing.

8

The rules

There aren't any, except these:

If you're not sure how to spell it, spell it how you say it.

If it's rude, it counts. Especially if it's rude.

If it's a word your family made up, it counts more.

If you can't remember the word but you remember when it was said, write that.
