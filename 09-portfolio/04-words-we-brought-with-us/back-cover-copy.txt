There are words only you still say.

The thing your mother shouted from the kitchen. The name for the soft part of the bread. The song you sang without knowing you were singing. The word that means tired, and happy, and a little sad, and evening, all at once, that has no English.

Your family has heard you say them their whole lives. They can't say them back. In a generation, nobody will.

The Words We Brought With Us is a keepsake for the part of a language that lived in one house. Fifty-two words, two to a page: the word, how to say it, what it means, when it was said, and who said it best. Then the sayings with a tone in them, the blessings and the mild curses, the songs with the words as they were actually sung, the food by its real name, and letters in both languages.

Any language. Any alphabet. Spelled the way you say it. A line on every entry for the one learning it to write how it sounds to them, and be corrected.

Written by the one who still has the words. Kept by the ones who want them back.
