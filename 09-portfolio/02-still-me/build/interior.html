If you're giving this book

Five things that make the difference between a book that gets used and one that stays in a drawer.

  1. Fill in the next page before you give it. Write who it's for and who's filling it in. A book with a name on it gets opened.
  2. Read Part One yourself first. It's short, and it will change how the first visit goes.
  3. Do the two-minute page in the second half before anything else. It's useful on day one.
  4. Bring three photographs to the first visit. The book explains why.
  5. Tell whoever else cares for them that the book exists, and where it lives.

Before you start

The person this book is about is still here. Some of what they know is going, and it's going in an order nobody chose: this morning first, and 1961 last. This book goes where the memory is strongest, and it writes down what it finds there, in their words, while they can still say them.

It has two halves. The first is the story of who they are, told to you, one page at a time, at the kitchen table or the bedside. The second is for whoever cares for them: what calms them, what upsets them, what they eat, what to say when they ask for someone who isn't coming.

Nothing in it has to be finished. A page with three words on it is a good page. A page left blank is an honest one.

Before you sit down with them, read the part called How to do this. It's fourteen pages, and it will make the first visit go better than it otherwise would. Then bring three photographs and a pen, and start with the house they grew up in.
