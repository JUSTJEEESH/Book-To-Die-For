# STILL ME — Brief (Stages 1 and 2)

**Title:** Still Me
**Tagline:** A life-story book, filled in together.
**Series:** Words to Keep · **Author:** Joshua Caleb Green
**Format:** 8.5 x 11 paperback, cream, matte, about 120 pages, no bleed. Hardcover same interior.
**Price:** $21.99 paperback, $29.99 hardcover.
**KDP:** Submit as a regular book. The first part is a real guide (about 7,500 words), so the book is
a guide with worksheets, not a prompt journal. If KDP reclassifies it as low-content, publish
without an ISBN like the others.
**Launch:** live by January 2027. No season. January is when families act on what they saw at Christmas.

## Positioning

Two books in one binding, filled in by the family with the person.

- **Part One, How to do this:** a plain, warm guide to life-story work with someone who is losing
  memory. How to ask, when to stop, what to do with a wrong answer, how to use the book with care
  staff, and a section for the person holding the pen.
- **Part Two, This is me:** the life story, in large type and short answers, in the person's own
  words when they can give them and in the family's transcription when they can't.
- **Part Three, What helps:** for anyone caring for them. A two-minute page at the front that care
  staff can read on a shift change, then the routine, what calms, what upsets, the names to say,
  what they were like before, what they can still do, and a dated log of what changed.

## Buyer and writer

- **Buyer:** the daughter (one in three dementia caregivers is a daughter), in the weeks after a
  diagnosis or at the move into memory care. Also activity directors, social workers, and hospice
  chaplains, who buy in fives and tens.
- **Writer:** the family, with the person. The person writes what they can. The book says so on
  every page that matters.
- **Completion:** designed for. Short answers, large type, one thing per page, permission to skip,
  and a guide that tells the family to stop before the person is tired. The two-minute page has
  value on day one even if nothing else is filled in.

## Differentiation

Clinical templates (Alzheimer's Society "This is me," Dementia UK "My Life Story") prove the
format is used daily in care settings; they are free PDFs. The KDP memory books are 6 x 9 prompt
lists with stock covers. "The Story of a Life" is a caregiver interview tool. Nothing combines the
life story, the caregiver half, a real guide, and a design a family would want to hold.

## Tone rules

Warmer than the flagship. Never clinical, never pitying, never a euphemism. "Losing memory," not
"journey." The person is "them" and "you," never "the patient" or "your loved one." No claims of
therapeutic effect; one plain sentence that life-story books are used in care settings and why.
Not a medical document, and says so.

## Structure (about 120 pages)

**Front (8):** half title · blank · title · copyright · *This book is for* (the person, filled in by,
started on) · *If you're giving this book* · *Before you start* (opening letter) · blank
**Part One — How to do this (about 18):** flowing text, sixteen short sections
**Part Two — This is me (about 44):** opener · about forty pages, one question each, with a photo box
on every other page · quick pages · a family question page
**Part Three — What helps (about 40):** opener · the two-minute page (2) · my day · what calms me ·
what upsets me, and how to tell · the names to say · food and drink · music · things I can still do ·
how I like to be helped · words we use · what I was like before · on a bad day · faith and rituals ·
sleep · pain and discomfort · people, with photographs (4) · what changed (a dated log, 4)
**Closing (4):** for the one reading this · blank pages · even count
