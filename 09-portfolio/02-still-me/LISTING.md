## Description (HTML)

<b>The person in this book is still here.</b>

Some of what they know is going, and it's going in an order nobody chose: this morning first, and 1961 last. <b>Still Me</b> goes where the memory is strongest and writes down what it finds there, in their words, while they can still say them.

<b>Two books in one binding, filled in together.</b>

<b>This is me:</b> their life story, one question to a page, in large type, with a place for a photograph on every page. The house they grew up in. Their mother. The work they did. The songs, and who sang them. The dog. Written by them where they can, and by the family where they can't.

<b>What helps:</b> for anyone who looks after them. A two-minute page a night nurse can read at a shift change. Their day. What calms them. What upsets them, and how to tell. The names they'll ask for, and what the family has agreed to say. Food, music, sleep, how they show pain, how they like to be helped. People and places with photographs. A dated log of what changed.

<b>It starts with a guide.</b> Fourteen pages on how to do this well: how to ask, when to stop, what to do with a wrong answer, what to say on the days they don't know you, and a page for the person holding the pen.

<ul>
<li>Forty-four life-story pages, large type, short answers, photo box on each</li>
<li>The two-minute page, plus twenty care pages built for home carers, care homes, hospitals, and hospice</li>
<li>The names to say: decide together, write it down, give everyone the same answer</li>
<li>Photograph pages for people and places, with names written large</li>
<li>A log of what changed, so the book stays current</li>
<li>Nothing has to be finished. A page with three words on it is a good page</li>
</ul>

Used the way life-story books have been used in care settings for years, and made to be held.

<b>Filled in together.</b>
