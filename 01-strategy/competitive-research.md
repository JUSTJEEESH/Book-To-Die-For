# Competitive Research — Legacy / Memory Journals on Amazon

Research date: September 2026. Sources are listed at the bottom. Amazon product pages block
automated fetching, so prices and page counts come from retailer pages, roundups, and
publisher sites. Verify live prices on Amazon before setting ours.

## 1. The market in one paragraph

This is a mature, crowded, gift-driven category with one dominant brand, a tier of
premium-materials hardcovers from traditional gift publishers, a long tail of low-effort
KDP clones, and a growing set of digital services (StoryWorth, Remento) that compete for
the same "preserve Mom's stories" dollar. Almost every physical title is written from the
child's point of view ("Mom, I want to hear your story", "Tell me your life story, Grandma").
Almost every one is chronological. Almost every one is split into gender/role variants.
Design is either floral-and-foil or plain KDP template. The emotional register is
nostalgic and safe.

## 2. Competitor set

### Tier A — the category leader

**Hear Your Story series — Jeffrey Mason** (Mom / Dad / Grandma / Grandpa / Nana / Papa / etc.)
- 21+ variants, 270,000+ copies sold worldwide, published since 2018, featured in Oprah Daily / USA Today.
- ~250 prompts, paperback ~$14.99, hardcover ~$19.95, multiple cover colorways ("Lavender", "Paradise") as separate ASINs.
- Origin story: author's father's Alzheimer's. Emotionally credible brand.
- Structure: chronological life stages, then favorites, then "words of wisdom."
- Criticism found: a 2-star review calls the questions "not bad exactly... but too dry" and says
  "the dry questions are unlikely to trigger deeper recall for the truly meaningful stories."
  Another reviewer: "some of the pages are really weird."
- Strategy takeaway: he wins on volume of variants and review accumulation, not on prompt quality.
  His variant strategy is copyable later. His prompt depth is beatable now.

### Tier B — premium-materials hardcovers (traditional gift publishers)

**Mom's Story / Dad's Story / Grandma's Story / Grandpa's Story — Korie Herold (Paige Tate & Co.)**
- $13–21, hardcover, gold foil + linen cover, archival paper. Very strong on shelf appeal.
- Prompts cover childhood, work, values, wisdom, regrets. Shorter, more curated.
- These are the "looks like an heirloom" benchmark. KDP cannot match linen or real foil.

**Grandparents Legacy Journal — Duncan & Stone Paper Co.** — $26–29, linen hardback, charcoal pages, family tree, blank pages at back.

**Memories For My Grandchild (Modern Classic edition)** — ~$15, cloth hardcover, silver foil, lay-flat binding, elastic closure, gender-neutral addressing, genealogy + era context.

**Grandma's / Grandpa's Journal** — $11–15, 96-page hardcover, colorful illustrations, back pocket, 4.8 stars.

**The Grandparents' Book of Memories** — ~$16, 100 questions, colorful flat-lay design.

### Tier C — KDP-native competitors

**Tell Me Your Life Story® series** (Questions About Me) — Grandma / Nana / Dad / Mom. ~$12, 119 pages, 200+ questions,
"quirky questions alongside standard life topics." Registered trademark on the series name.

**Grandparents, Tell Me Your Story** (140 questions), **A Lifetime of Memories** (David Hough), **A Grandparent's Book — My Life My Story**,
**My Life, My Story** (105 prompts, large print, "for seniors"), **Grandma, tell me your story — a guided memory journal** (2026),
**My Story: A Guided Memory Journal for Parents and Grandparents** (2026), **Grandparents' Memory Journal**.

Pattern: template covers (florals, watercolor, stock photos), "N questions" on the cover, 100–140 pages,
prompts share pages, chronological, interchangeable.

**Questions You'll Wish You Asked: A Time Capsule Journal for Grandparents and Grandchildren — Melissa Pennel.**
Important: this title already owns the exact phrase "questions you'll wish you asked." Our marketing
must not lean on that phrasing. Note it uses "time capsule" too.

### Tier D — digital services (substitutes, not direct competitors)

- **StoryWorth** — $59 / $109 / $199 per year, weekly email prompts, hardcover book at year end.
  Documented complaints: recipients "answer three weekly emails and then quietly stop" because typing
  is friction for people in their 70s and 80s; price escalates to ~$206 with add-ons; single storyteller only.
- **Remento** ($99+), **KindredTales** ($90–250), **Storii**, **Memorygram** (4.8 stars; "takes longer than expected").
- Physical journals are described in these comparisons as "50–75 prompts covering basic topics" in
  "smaller formats (5x7 or 6x8 inches)" and "a lighter, less demanding option."

## 3. Recurring complaints across the category (what we must solve)

| Complaint | Where it shows up | Our design response |
|---|---|---|
| Questions are "dry," generic, don't trigger real memories | Mason 2-star review; roundup commentary ("most pre-made lists fail because they're generic") | Every prompt must pass the 9-test filter; specificity over breadth; "the questions nobody thinks to ask" as the signature layer |
| Chronological biography feels like a form | Structural pattern across Tier A and C | Emotional arc, not timeline. Chronology only in Parts 1–2, then the book turns inward |
| Not enough writing space; small formats | Comparison sites; 5x7 and 6x8 formats common; prompts stacked 2–3 per page | 7x10, one prompt per page minimum, spreads for major prompts |
| Recipient never finishes it | StoryWorth complaints; "many remain unfinished" | Low-friction opening, permission to skip, "quick ones" pages, start-anywhere design, giver's note for accountability |
| Childish or template design | Tier C covers | Typographic, minimal, modern-heirloom cover; no florals, hearts, cursive, foil effects |
| Gendered variants force the buyer to choose and split the family | Tier A and C | Gender-neutral flagship; one ASIN concentrates reviews |
| Surface-level: never gets to feelings, regrets, the unsaid | Implicit in every competitor's table of contents | Parts 5–13 are the product |
| Digital services require typing/tech | StoryWorth abandonment | Handwriting is the product. The hand is the heirloom |

## 4. What no competitor does

1. Speaks in the writer's voice. Every title says "tell me." Ours says "I want you to know."
2. Has a formal section for what was never said.
3. Has a "when you miss me" section that preserves connection without being a memorial.
4. Lets the gift-giver write a note and add their own questions before giving it.
5. Treats the ending as a letter rather than a "words of wisdom" list.
6. Designs the object as literary nonfiction rather than a gift-shop journal.
