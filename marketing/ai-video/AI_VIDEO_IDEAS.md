# AI VIDEO IDEAS
## WHAT I WANT YOU TO KNOW (Words to Keep)

Twenty concepts for short-form AI-assisted video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, and paid social), built from specific lines in the manuscript. Each concept is production-ready: hook, shot list, verbatim text, verbatim voiceover, music, edit notes, CTA, caption, pinned comment, rationale, book connection, and per-shot prompts for Veo, Sora, Runway and Kling.

## PRINCIPLES FOR AI VIDEO IN THIS BRAND

**1. Conceptual, never documentary.**
AI people in our videos are characters in a short film, the way an actor in a car commercial is a character. They are never presented as a customer, a reviewer, a real family, or the author. When a video could be mistaken for a real family, the text says so in our voice: "Not your dad. But you have one." That line is not a disclaimer. It is the point of the ad.

**2. No fake testimonials. No invented user stories.**
We never show an AI person holding the book saying it changed anything. We never show "Margaret, 74, Ohio." We never write "my mom filled this in and cried." The book is a blank object until a real person writes in it; our videos show the space, not the result.

**3. Realism level is a creative choice, and we pick it on purpose.**
- *Filmic photoreal* (35mm grain, period-correct, shallow depth) for memory scenes. Aim for "this looks like a movie about someone's dad," not "this looks like footage of someone's dad."
- *Home-video texture* (VHS, Super 8, disposable-camera flash) is allowed only when the on-screen text clearly frames it as a scene, never as found footage.
- *Object photoreal* (hands, paper, kitchens, phones, dishes) can go fully realistic. Objects don't lie about who they are.
- *Typographic* (text on paper, no people) is our safest and often strongest register.
- Never uncanny. If a face drifts between shots, cut the shot rather than ship it.

**4. Where AI beats real footage.**
Period scenes (1954, 1971, 1979, 1991) at a cost we could never shoot. The same face at 19 and at 64. A kitchen across twenty years. A single unbroken shot where daylight crosses a room. Fast iteration on twelve versions of a hook.

**5. Where AI loses to real footage, and we use real footage instead.**
The book itself. Every close-up of a page, the cover, the spine, the "given to" page, the signature page, is shot on a phone or a proper camera with the real product. AI tools render text badly and books worse. Every concept below has a "real insert" shot for that reason. Real handwriting also beats generated handwriting; when a page needs writing on it, we write on it.

**6. The four hard rules, restated for video.**
- Death may be in the room. It is never the topic. No hospital corridors, no funerals, no urns, no headstones, no "last time."
- No clocks, no countdowns, no sand, no candles burning down.
- Banned language in any text, voiceover, caption or comment: "create memories that last forever", "capture every precious moment", "celebrate the journey", "before it's too late", "before they're gone".
- Nobody is dying in these videos. Everybody is alive, specific, and slightly funny.

**7. Honesty line, when needed.**
When an AI person carries the emotional weight of a video, one of these appears on screen or in the first line of the caption:
- "Not your dad. But you have one."
- "Not your mom. But you have one."
- "Not your grandmother. Yours had a bedroom at seventeen too."
- "Not a real family. Yours is."
Pick the one that fits. Never more than one per video. Platforms that require an AI-content label get it; we set the label rather than hide it.

**8. Prompt house style.**
Every shot prompt below carries the same labeled fields so nothing is missed: AR, LENGTH, CAMERA, LENS, LIGHT, ENV, PERIOD, WARDROBE, DIRECTION, FACE, PROPS, TRANSITION, REALISM, NEGATIVE, CONTINUITY. Character and set sheets are written once per concept in square brackets, e.g. [SHEET AV-01 DAD], and every prompt for that concept says to paste the sheet verbatim at the top. Do not paraphrase the sheet between shots. Paraphrasing is how faces drift.

**9. General tool adjustments (apply to every prompt).**
- *Veo*: renders native audio; add "no dialogue, no speech" unless a shot calls for it. Best at 8 s clips; ask for 8 and trim. Put the sheet first, then action.
- *Sora*: handles longer takes and multi-beat storyboards; use it for the single unbroken shot and the montage. Weak with text on props; keep pages blank.
- *Runway (Gen-4 and later)*: strongest path to consistent faces is image-to-video. Generate a still of the character from the sheet first, approve it, then animate every shot from that still. Use 5 s or 10 s.
- *Kling*: use the reference/elements feature with the approved still. Good with hands and kitchens. Use "extend" for anything past 10 s.
- All tools: hands are still the weak point. Keep hand actions simple (hold, turn a page, lift a dish, dial). If a shot needs a pen touching paper in close-up, shoot it for real.

**10. Default negative block (paste into every prompt).**
"No on-screen text, no captions, no subtitles, no logos, no brand marks, no watermarks. No extra fingers, no fused fingers, no distorted hands. No extra limbs. No modern objects in period scenes (no smartphones, no flat screens, no LED bulbs, no plastic water bottles, no modern cars). No clocks, no calendars with readable dates, no hospital settings, no funeral settings. No warping faces, no morphing between people, no floating objects, no lens flare unless asked."

## AV-01. DAD WAS 19

**Structure:** nostalgia, then a hard cut. **Length:** 22 s. **People:** one AI character at two ages. **Real inserts:** shot 5.

### HOOK
Text on screen at 0 s, over a young man laughing in a parking lot at night: **"Your dad was 19 once."**

### VIDEO CONCEPT
Three quiet moments from one summer night in 1978, seen through a young man we never name. He isn't doing anything dramatic. He's leaning on a car, standing at a payphone deciding whether to call someone, walking home at dawn with his jacket over his shoulder. Then one hard cut: the same face, forty-six years later, at a kitchen table with a pen, the book open to a "Finish these" page. He has never told anyone about that night. He might write it. The ad is the cut.

### SHOT LIST
1. 0 to 4 s. Young man leans against the hood of a 1970s sedan under a sodium streetlight, laughing at someone off-frame.
2. 4 to 8 s. Payphone outside a closed gas station. He holds the receiver, coin in the other hand, rehearses something, hangs up without dialing.
3. 8 to 12 s. Walking down a suburban road at first light, jacket over his shoulder, sprinklers going.
4. 12 to 18 s. Hard cut. The same man at 65, kitchen at 7 a.m., the book open, pen hovering. He starts to write.
5. 18 to 22 s. Real insert. Close on the printed page: "At nineteen, I was sure that" and the blank rule beneath. Then the cover.

### ON-SCREEN TEXT
1. "Your dad was 19 once."
2. "There's a night he's never told you about."
3. "He'd never say it out loud."
4. "He might write it."
5. "At nineteen, I was sure that ______"
6. "WHAT I WANT YOU TO KNOW. A book he fills in by hand."
7. "Not your dad. But you have one."

### VOICEOVER
none

### MUSIC/SOUND DIRECTION
An original, late-70s-feeling clean electric guitar figure, slow, mono, slightly tape-warm. Under shot 1: distant car radio, crickets. Shot 2: the receiver's dial tone, coins in a palm. Shot 3: sprinklers, one bird. On the cut to shot 4 the music drops to nothing and we hear only the kitchen: a fridge hum, a pen on paper. The guitar returns, quieter, for the cover.

### EDITING NOTES
No dissolves. Shots 1 to 3 are straight cuts on movement. Shot 3 to 4 is a hard cut on a matched head position (young man looking down-left at the road, older man looking down-left at the page). Text is set in the book's typeface, cream on black, lower third, one line at a time, no animation beyond a 6-frame fade. Hold the honesty line for a full 2 s at the end. Grade shots 1 to 3 warm and slightly lifted blacks; shot 4 neutral and clean.

### CTA
"On Amazon. Link in bio. Give it to him, then ask him one question out loud."

### CAPTION
He was 19 before he was Dad. There's a page in this book that starts "At nineteen, I was sure that" and he gets to finish it, by hand. Not your dad. But you have one. Link in bio. #legacyjournal #dadgift #whatiwantyoutoknow #fathersday #familystories

### COMMENTS TO PIN
"What's the one story you know your dad has never told you the whole of? (Don't tell us the story. Just tell us you know it exists.)"

### WHY IT COULD WORK
The gap between "Dad" and "a 19-year-old at a payphone" is a feeling every adult child already carries. We don't explain it; we cut on it. The payphone beat (hangs up without dialing) plants a story without telling it, which is exactly what the book sells: the untold version. The honesty line protects us and, oddly, makes the ad feel more trustworthy than a real family would.

### BOOK CONNECTION
Part Two, "Becoming Me": opener ("Somewhere between the child and the adult, there was a person your family has never met"), the "Finish these" page ("At nineteen, I was sure that / And I was right about"), and "Tell me about leaving home. What you didn't say."

## AV-02. THE EMPTY CHAIR

**Structure:** the single unbroken shot; suspense that resolves into humor. **Length:** 20 s. **People:** one child, seen from behind only. **Real inserts:** none needed; optional cover tag.

### HOOK
Text at 0 s over a kitchen chair in early light: **"Don't keep the house like a museum."**

### VIDEO CONCEPT
One kitchen chair at one table. The camera never moves and never cuts. Daylight travels across the room from morning to late afternoon in a single continuous take, the way it does when nobody's home. Nothing happens for eighteen seconds. Then a kid pulls the chair out, sits, and eats cereal in it, badly. The text carries the line from the book: don't keep the house like a museum. The chair is for sitting in.

### SHOT LIST
1. 0 to 20 s. One unbroken locked-off shot. A wooden chair at a kitchen table. Light crosses the wall. At 17 s, a child (9 or 10, seen only from behind and in profile) drags the chair out, climbs on, pours cereal, sits sideways with one knee up.
2. Optional 2 s tag. Real insert of the cover.

### ON-SCREEN TEXT
1. "Don't keep the house like a museum."
2. "Sit in the chair."
3. "Use the good plates."
4. "Play the song. Skip the one that wrecks you."
5. "There's a page in the book where they tell you what not to do."
6. "WHAT I WANT YOU TO KNOW"

### VOICEOVER
none

### MUSIC/SOUND DIRECTION
No music until the chair moves. Room tone only: a fridge, a distant lawnmower, the house ticking as it warms. At 17 s: chair legs scraping tile, a cereal box, milk, a spoon. A single warm upright-piano chord under the last two text cards, then out.

### EDITING NOTES
This is the one video where the discipline is the concept: do not cut, do not zoom, do not add a slow push in post. If the AI tool can't hold 20 s, generate as two or three "extend" passes and stitch on the same frame; the stitch must be invisible or the concept fails. Text cards appear at 0, 5, 9, 13, 18 s, lower third, no animation. The child must never turn to face the camera. Let the cereal be a little loud.

### CTA
"Link in bio. The page is in Part Twelve. It's called 'What I don't want you to do.'"

### CAPTION
There's a page in this book where a parent writes down what they don't want you to do. The book offers a starting line: "Don't keep the house like a museum." The rest is theirs to fill in. #legacyjournal #familyhome #whatiwantyoutoknow #grandparents #giftideas

### COMMENTS TO PIN
"The book's version starts: 'Don't keep the house like a museum. Don't ______.' What would your mom or dad put in that blank?"

### WHY IT COULD WORK
Eighteen seconds of an empty chair is a pattern interrupt on a feed built on cuts. People stay because nothing is happening and they want to know why. The payoff is a kid eating cereal, which is funnier and more honest than anything sentimental. The book line does the emotional work without saying the word "grief" once.

### BOOK CONNECTION
Part Twelve, "When You Miss Me": the marked prompt "What I don't want you to do. Don't keep the house like a museum. Don't ______." and the opener ("This isn't a sad section. It's a practical one.").

## AV-03. THE PHOTOGRAPH

**Structure:** curiosity, with a deliberate refusal of the obvious AI trick. **Length:** 24 s. **People:** three young women in a still photograph, one older hand. **Real inserts:** shot 5.

### HOOK
Text at 0 s over a photo on a fridge: **"Every fridge has one of these."**

### VIDEO CONCEPT
A black-and-white snapshot, 1954, three young women on a beach, arms around each other, squinting. It's pinned to a fridge with a magnet. We push into it, slowly, the way you do when you finally look. The text tells us who two of them are. Nobody in the family knows the third. The photo never moves, never "comes alive," and that restraint is the concept: the book is where you find out, not the algorithm. An older hand takes the photo off the fridge and tapes it into a page in the book, and writes a name under the third woman.

### SHOT LIST
1. 0 to 4 s. Fridge door, a magnet, the photo among others.
2. 4 to 10 s. Slow push into the photograph. Grain, silver, sand.
3. 10 to 14 s. Older hand takes the photo down.
4. 14 to 20 s. Hand lays the photo on a blank page of the open book and smooths a piece of tape across the corner.
5. 20 to 24 s. Real insert. The real book's "photo" page, a real photo taped in, a pen writing a name.

### ON-SCREEN TEXT
1. "Every fridge has one of these."
2. "Three girls. 1954. Somewhere with sand."
3. "The one on the left is your grandmother."
4. "The one in the middle is her sister."
5. "Nobody knows who the third one is."
6. "She does. Ask."
7. "Then tape it in."
8. "WHAT I WANT YOU TO KNOW. Blank pages included, for exactly this."

### VOICEOVER
none

### MUSIC/SOUND DIRECTION
A single sustained cello note, then a second one a fifth up, held under the push-in. Fridge hum. The magnet's click. Paper on paper. The tape pulling off the roll is the loudest sound in the piece. No music under the real insert; just the pen.

### EDITING NOTES
The push into the photograph must be a real optical push, not a Ken Burns crop, and the photo must stay a photo: no parallax, no blinking, no animated faces. If a tool animates the women, throw the clip out. Text lines 3 to 5 land on the push-in, one per second. Line 6 ("She does. Ask.") gets a full 2 s hold on the still. Cut to shot 3 on the hand entering. Match the photo across shots 2 to 5 by generating the photograph as a still image first and compositing the same file into every shot.

### CTA
"Link in bio. There's a family tree page in the book too. Question marks are allowed."

### CAPTION
The photo on the fridge. You know two of them. Nobody knows the third. She does. This book has blank pages and a family tree where question marks are allowed. #familyphotos #legacyjournal #whatiwantyoutoknow #grandmother #genealogy

### COMMENTS TO PIN
"Who's the person in your family photos that nobody can name anymore? Go ask. Report back."

### WHY IT COULD WORK
It exploits the thing everyone expects from AI (the photo will animate) and denies it, which makes people watch to the end waiting for it. The unnamed third woman is a real, common, low-stakes mystery in most families. The CTA is a task, not a purchase, and the book is the place the task ends up.

### BOOK CONNECTION
Part One: "Describe your grandparents to someone who never met them," the family-tree special page ("Fill in what you know. Question marks are allowed."), and the photo pages that close several parts. "How to use this book": "Blank pages are for photographs, drawings, recipes."
