# Brand Voice: rules for every caption, script, ad, reply, and email

## How it sounds

Human. Warm. Smart. Specific. Slightly nostalgic. Occasionally funny. A person, not a publisher.
Plain sentences. Short ones. The book's own voice is the model: "Not the title. The Tuesday."

## Always

- Quote the manuscript. Its prompts are better than anything we will write about them.
- Be specific. A first car, a stupid haircut, a worst boss, a phone number still known by heart.
- Let the emotion come from the premise. The premise is enough.
- Give permission. Skipping is allowed. Ten pages is a treasure. Nobody finishes a book like this.
- Make the viewer want to send it to someone. That is the whole test.
- Say who the book is for by relationship, never by age or health.

## Never

- "Create memories that last forever." "Capture every precious moment." "Celebrate the journey." "Cherish." "Treasure" (as a verb). "Heartfelt." "Priceless." "Legacy" in organic copy (allowed in Amazon metadata only).
- "Before it's too late." "Before they're gone." "While you still can." Any countdown. Any clock.
- Death as the topic. It is present the way it is at a good family dinner.
- Fake testimonials. Invented stories. Fabricated comments. Manufactured scarcity. "This book changed my life" unless a real, named person said it and we have permission.
- "Bestseller," "number one," "thousands of families," until Amazon's badge or real numbers exist.
- An author's face or name as the reason to buy.
- Em-dashes. Use a period.
- Exclamation points in the brand's own voice. (Quoted people may use them.)
- Stock-photo families. Hands, paper, pens, kitchens, real handwriting, real old photographs with permission.

## Words we use for the product

"The book." "A book they fill in by hand." "For the people who will keep it." "Made to be written
in, and kept." "One prompt to a page." "The questions nobody thinks to ask."

## Words we use for the people

"Your mom, your dad, your grandma, your grandpa, the person who raised you." Never "elderly,"
"seniors," "aging loved ones." Never "the recipient."

## Reply voice

Short. Warm. Ask one question back. Never sell in a reply to a story about someone's dead parent.
If someone shares a loss, the reply is about them, not the book. The pinned comment can carry the
link. Replies carry the conversation.

## Read-aloud test

Every line is read aloud before it posts. If it would embarrass a 70-year-old to say to their own
child, cut it.
