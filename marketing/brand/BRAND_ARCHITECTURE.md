# Brand Architecture: five options, one recommendation

The author is not the face. The brand has to carry the audience, the book, and every product after
it. Scored on: audience growth potential, memorability, emotional resonance, searchability,
scalability beyond this book, and ability to sell multiple products. 1 to 5 each, 30 max.

## The five candidates

### 1. Words to Keep (the existing imprint)

Already printed on the spine, the copyright page, and the Amazon listing. Plain, warm, gift-shaped.

| Growth | Memory | Emotion | Search | Scale | Multi-product | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | **25** |

Strengths: it means the content (the questions and answers are the words) and the product line
(a Mom edition, cards, a couples book, an audio version are all "words to keep"). It does not
date, does not sound like a campaign, and does not tie the brand to parents only, so a grandparent
book, a couples book, or a children's book fit under it. It is on 254 pages of product already.

Weaknesses: generic words, so brand search is weak until people know it. Handle may be taken on
some platforms (fallbacks: @words.to.keep, @wordstokeep.co, @keepthewords). Slightly quiet as a hook.

### 2. The Questions We Never Ask

| Growth | Memory | Emotion | Search | Scale | Multi-product | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | **23** |

The best description of the content. It is what the account posts. Highly shareable as a title.
But it is a series, not a house: a recipe book or a couples book does not sit under it, and
"never ask" carries a faint accusation. Best use: the flagship *series* name inside the brand.

### 3. Your Parents Were People Too

| Growth | Memory | Emotion | Search | Scale | Multi-product | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | **21** |

The most viral phrase of the set. Funny, true, meme-shaped. It will do numbers as a series and as a
hashtag. As a brand it excludes grandparents, spouses, and self-writers, and it would age badly the
moment the audience is mostly over 50. Use it as a series and a sound, not a name.

### 4. What I Want You to Know (the book title as the brand)

| Growth | Memory | Emotion | Search | Scale | Multi-product | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3 | 5 | 2 | 3 | 3 | **19** |

Emotionally the strongest phrase we own, and it is the product. But it is a common sentence
(unsearchable, and there are memoirs and songs with the name), it makes every post read as an ad
for one SKU, and it cannot hold a second book with a different title. The book keeps the title. The
brand does not.

### 5. Before You Forget / Ask Your Parent / Things My Parents Never Told Me

| Growth | Memory | Emotion | Search | Scale | Multi-product | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 2 | **17** |

"Before You Forget" breaks the brand's own rule (no clock, no "before it's too late") and reads as a
warning. "Ask Your Parent" is a good handle for an instructional account and a bad one for a
grandparent product. "Things My Parents Never Told Me" implies secrets kept from you, which is
darker than the book, and it is first-person, so it needs a person.

## Recommendation: a two-layer architecture

**House brand: Words to Keep.** The account name, the bio, the watermark, the profile image, the
future product line, the Amazon brand (when Brand Registry becomes possible), the email sender.

**Content identity: "The questions nobody thinks to ask."** This is the book's own phrase (the marked
prompts). It is the bio line, the recurring on-screen sign-off, and the name of the flagship series.
It tells a new follower exactly what they will get.

**Series brands underneath:** Not As a Mother · Your Parents Were People Too · The Version That
Actually Happened · When You Miss Me · Not the Title, the Tuesday · Ten Pages · Things I Never Said.
Each can carry a hashtag and a sound. None of them needs a face.

**Product name stays:** What I Want You to Know, "from Words to Keep."

### Why this and not one name

One name has to do two jobs that pull apart: describe the content (questions, stories, the unsaid)
and hold the products (a book, an edition, cards, audio). Words to Keep holds the products and is
already printed. The content line does the describing. Together they read as a small publisher
with a point of view, which is what the audience should believe it is following.

### Tone in three sentences

We sound like a person at a kitchen table who has done this before and is not in a hurry. We quote
the book instead of paraphrasing it into a greeting card. We are allowed to be funny, and we are
never allowed to use a clock.
