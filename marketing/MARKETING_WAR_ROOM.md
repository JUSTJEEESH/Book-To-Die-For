# MARKETING WAR ROOM: What I Want You to Know

Central command for the faceless brand that sells this book. Built 2026-09-21, the day after the
book went live. Everything in this folder answers one question: how do we make people realize there
are entire parts of the people they love that they have never asked about, and then hand them a
beautiful way to do something about it.

The book is the product. The questions are the content. The stories are the marketing. The emotion
is the distribution. The audience is the long-term asset.

## The one-page strategy

1. **Position.** Every competitor asks "tell me your story" or sells "hear their voice." We own the
   questions themselves, and handwriting. Not "tell me your story." Their words, for you.
2. **Brand.** Faceless. Words to Keep is the publisher; the account posts the questions nobody thinks
   to ask, one a night, and what people's parents said back. The author's name stays on the cover and
   nowhere else.
3. **Content.** Quote the book. Eighteen pillars, ten flagship series, a nightly question. At most
   one post in five is about the book. Content → emotion → conversation → profile → book.
4. **Distribution.** Sends and shares, not views. The impulse we build for is "I need to send this to
   Mom." Grief content is the sharing engine; we never sell into it.
5. **Conversion.** The Amazon page does the selling: seven images, seven A+ modules, first twenty
   reviews, the description's first line. Every link is an Amazon Attribution link so we know what sold.
6. **Paid.** Organic winners become ads. Nothing scales before twenty reviews. Break-even is about $6
   per attributed paperback sale. Christmas and Mother's Day get the budget.
7. **Creators.** Real parents, real handwriting, honest reactions. Gifting first, micro-creators
   first, hospice and genealogy educators as the credibility layer.
8. **Learning.** Daily workflow produces; weekly review decides; the database remembers. The engine
   never posts the same idea twice with different words.
9. **Products.** Audience first. Nothing new launches before 100 reviews on the flagship. The
   roadmap is in `future-products/ROADMAP.md`.
10. **Honesty.** No fake testimonials, no invented stories, no fabricated comments, no scarcity, no
    "bestseller" until Amazon says so. The emotion comes from the premise. It is enough.

## Workflows

- `/daily-marketing` produces the day's ideas and names the three most worth making today
  (`.claude/skills/daily-marketing/SKILL.md`).
- `/weekly-marketing` runs the review and updates the database, the lab, the winners and failures
  (`.claude/skills/weekly-marketing/SKILL.md`).

## The content factory

```
MANUSCRIPT (04-manuscript/MANUSCRIPT.md)
  → PILLAR (content/PILLARS.md)
    → IDEA (CONTENT_DATABASE.md, with an ID)
      → HOOK (content/HOOKS.md; logged so it is not repeated)
        → SCRIPT (scripts/, or the package format in the daily skill)
          → VISUAL CONCEPT (image-content/, ai-video/, or real footage via creators/)
            → PRODUCTION (text card in 15 min; object shoot in 1 h; creator in a week)
              → POST (accounts/ACCOUNT_STRATEGY.md cadence; sign-off frame; pinned comment)
                → MEASURE (analytics/METRICS.md; Attribution tags)
                  → LEARN (WEEKLY_MARKETING.md; winning-content/; failed-content/)
                    → REMIX (content/TESTING_MATRIX.md; the next version)
                      → AD TEST (ads/PAID_ADS.md, stage 1 to 3)
                        → AMAZON SALE (amazon/AMAZON_CONVERSION.md)
```

Templates for each stage: the database entry (CONTENT_DATABASE.md, "Fields per entry"); the
production package (scripts/ and the daily skill's section 30 format); the version matrix
(TESTING_MATRIX.md); the review entry (WEEKLY_MARKETING.md template); the ad card (PAID_ADS.md,
seven fields); the experiment row (EXPERIMENTS.md).

## Rules that do not change

- Nothing invented. Not a comment, not a reaction, not a family.
- No clock. No "before it's too late."
- The book appears in at most one post in five.
- Every video ends on the sign-off frame.
- Every link is an Attribution link.
- A comment about a loss is answered as a person, never as a store.
- The repository name never appears in anything public.
