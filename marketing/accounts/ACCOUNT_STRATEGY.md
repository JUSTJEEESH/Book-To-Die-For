# Account Strategy

The recommendation (`../brand/BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md`) is one house brand, Words to Keep, on every
platform, with the content line "The questions nobody thinks to ask." Three alternative account
strategies are documented below so the decision is explicit, and a fourth, the recommended one, is
fully specified.

## Option A (recommended): one brand, every platform, one voice

### ACCOUNT NAME
Words to Keep

### HANDLE IDEAS
@wordstokeep · @words.to.keep · @wordstokeepbooks · @keepthewords. Check all four on TikTok,
Instagram, YouTube, Pinterest, Facebook, Threads, and X the same afternoon; register the same one
everywhere even where it will not be used for a year.

### BIO
- TikTok: The questions nobody thinks to ask your parents. Ask one tonight.
- Instagram: The questions nobody thinks to ask. Their words, in their handwriting, for you. The book is below.
- YouTube: We make books your parents and grandparents fill in by hand, and we post the questions that get them talking.
- Pinterest: Questions to ask your parents and grandparents, and a book they write the answers in.
- Facebook: A small publisher of books your family fills in by hand. We post one question a night.

### PROFILE IMAGE CONCEPT
The cover's mark: a cream ring (the marked-prompt sign from the book) on ink #1e2838. No face. No
photo. Identical on every platform.

### CONTENT PILLARS
All eighteen in `../content/PILLARS.md`, weighted by platform:
- TikTok: 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 14 (curious and funny; grandchildren and daughters)
- Instagram: 1, 6, 7, 12, 15, 18 (saves and shares; daughters)
- Facebook: 8, 9, 13, 17 (the 55+ audience; self-writers; shares)
- Pinterest: 1, 12, 18 (lists; gift planning; evergreen search)
- YouTube: 1, 18 and the two long-form pieces (C-20, C-49)
- Threads: 7, 9, 12 (text-only stems and questions)

### AUDIENCE
Segments A (daughters 35-55), B (grandchildren 20-35), C (the ones who waited too long), D
(self-writers 55+), E (new parents), F (caregivers), G (professionals). See
`../research/POSITIONING_ANALYSIS.md`.

### WHY PEOPLE FOLLOW
They get one question a night that they can actually use with their own parents, and they get to
read what other people's parents said. Nobody else posts that. The book is the reason the account
exists, not the reason to follow it.

### HOW THE BOOK ENTERS THE CONTENT
- The sign-off frame on every video (1.5 s): "The questions nobody thinks to ask. Words to Keep."
- The pinned comment: "There's a page for this in the book. Link in bio."
- The link page: the book (Amazon Attribution link), the ten questions as a free PDF, the email list.
- One overt product post in five, at most.
- Every prompt quoted on screen is a page in the book, so every post is a sample.

### POSTING FREQUENCY
- TikTok: 1 a day (S10 "One Question Tonight" counts), 2 on weekends.
- Instagram: 4 Reels a week (cross-posted from TikTok without the watermark), 2 carousels, daily Stories (the poll, the question).
- Facebook: 1 a day (text and static; the older audience; cross-post the quietest videos).
- Pinterest: 5 pins a week, all evergreen lists and product stills.
- YouTube: Shorts daily (the TikToks), one long video a month.
- Threads: the nightly question as text.

### MONETIZATION
1. The book (paperback, hardcover) through Amazon Attribution links.
2. Amazon Associates on the same links once the account qualifies (a few percent; not the point).
3. The email list (the ten questions PDF) for launch days and Mother's Day.
4. Later: the editions and the portfolio books; the conversation-cards product; a printable.

### LONG-TERM PRODUCT POTENTIAL
An audience that trusts this account to hand them a good question will buy: a Mom edition, a Dad
edition, a couples book, cards for the dinner table, a grandparent book, a Spanish edition, an audio
guided version, and a "read this when" letters book. All under Words to Keep. None launched before
the flagship has 100 reviews (the decision in `../../01-strategy/DECISIONS.md`).

## Option B: a content brand with its own name, the book as a "partner"

Account: **The Questions We Never Ask** (@questionsweneverask). Bio: "One question a night for the
people you love." The book appears as "our favorite place to write the answers."

Why it could work: the name is the content; it is more followable than a publisher name; it can
feature other people's products (which builds trust) and later sell its own.
Why not now: two brands to build with one person's time; a mismatch between the account name and
the product name at the moment of purchase; and the book's spine already says Words to Keep.

Keep as: the name of the flagship series inside Option A.

## Option C: relationship-specific accounts

Accounts: @askyourdad, @askyourmom, @askyourgrandma. Each posts only for its relationship.

Why it could work: search-shaped handles; each account's follower is precisely a buyer.
Why not now: three times the posting load; audiences overlap; the funniest content (Dad) and the
most shared (Mom) would be split; and the book is one book.

Keep as: series names (S1 Not As a Mother, S2 Your Parents Were People Too, S8 Ask Your Grandparent
This) and hashtags.

## Option D: a person-led account with a hired host

A named host (not the author) reads questions to camera, interviews their own parents, and becomes
the face.

Why it could work: faces outperform text on TikTok; a host's parents are a renewable content source.
Why not now: the brief says faceless; a host is a dependency and a cost; the brand becomes theirs.

Keep as: the creator strategy. Creators are the faces, temporarily and plurally, and the brand stays
the brand.

## Operating rules for the recommended account

1. **Nothing invented.** Every "what you told us" post is built from real comments with permission asked in the reply thread. First names only. If there are eight, post eight.
2. **Reply protocol.** In `../content/COMMENT_STRATEGY.md`. No selling under a loss.
3. **Sound.** Original audio (pen on paper, page turn, a permissioned voice memo) as the signature; Commercial Music Library piano for anything that might be boosted. Do not post from a Business account on TikTok until the sound question is settled; a Creator account keeps licensed sounds available for organic posts.
4. **Sign-off frame** on every video. No exceptions.
5. **Weekly review** in `../WEEKLY_MARKETING.md`. Kill, keep, repeat.
6. **Two hours a day** is the realistic budget: one for making (S10 takes ten minutes; one bigger piece takes an hour), one for replying and logging.
