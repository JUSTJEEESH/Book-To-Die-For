## 2. Image carousel (seven slots after the cover)

All photographs of the real printed book. Real handwriting from a real parent or grandparent, with
permission. Never a mock-up. Shoot at 2,000 px or larger on the long side, on a plain surface,
daylight.

1. **Cover, front, on white.** Amazon's requirement for the first image.
2. **An open prompt page with a handwritten answer** ("Tell me about your mother, not as a mother."), shot from above, a pen resting on the page. This is the image that tells a video viewer "yes, this is the one."
3. **The giver's page, filled in:** "This book was given to Mom by Sarah, on Mother's Day, because…" Real first names (or the team's own, honestly).
4. **The Part Seven opener, Things I Never Said,** with two of its prompts visible.
5. **A quick-ones page half filled in.** Shows how easy it is to start.
6. **The last letter page, empty, pen laid diagonally** ("Four pages. No prompt."). Also the retargeting image.
7. **Typographic infographic in the cover's colors:** 13 parts · 202 prompts · one prompt to a page · 7 x 10 inches · 254 pages · 8 letters · a page for the family's own questions · an index. Then the hardcover and paperback side by side.

## 3. A+ Content (seven modules)

1. **Hero banner.** The ink cover on a linen table, open to a written-in page. Headline: *Not "tell me your story." Their words, for you.*
2. **Comparison chart** (Amazon's standard module). Rows: written in the parent's voice · one prompt per page · the family adds its own questions · a section for when you miss them · ends with letters · one book for Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa. Our column checks every row. The other column is "typical memory journals." No competitor named.
3. **Four-image grid.** The giver's page filled in four ways (ST-07). Caption: *One book. Every one of them.*
4. **Text plus image.** A spread prompt photographed. *The questions nobody thinks to ask.* Three quoted: "Who do you still talk to in your head?" "What did people in your family not talk about?" "And the risk you didn't take."
5. **Text plus image.** The When You Miss Me opener. *This isn't a sad section. It's a practical one.* Two quick ones quoted: "My coffee order." "What to do on my birthday."
6. **Text plus image.** The last letter, open and blank. *One last letter. Four pages. No prompt.*
7. **Brand module.** Words to Keep, three sentences: what the series is for, that every book is made to be written in by hand, and that nothing inside assumes who the writer is to you. No claims.

## 6. Strongest selling points, in the order buyers care

1. It is in their voice, and it ends with a letter to you.
2. The questions are ones you have never asked (the marked prompts).
3. There is real room to write: one prompt to a page, big type, 7 x 10.
4. You, the giver, write into it too (the giver's page, the family-question pages).
5. It is honest about completion: ten pages is a treasure.
6. One book works for every parent and grandparent.
7. There is a section for the days you miss them that is practical, not sad.

## 10. Reviews, done inside Amazon's rules

- Gifted copies may be reviewed if the reviewer discloses the gift and the review is unconditional. Never ask for a positive review; never ask family in the same household.
- Twenty-five early readers, author copies at print cost, a note that says: "If it means something to you, an honest review helps another family find it."
- No review-swap groups, no incentives, no "review for a refund."
- The book's own words do the asking: the pinned comment under the account's product posts can say "if you've written in one, tell us how it went," and the reply can point to the Amazon page.

## 11. What we will not do on the page

images of families. No render in place of a photograph. No countdown language.
