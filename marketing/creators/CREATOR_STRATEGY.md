# Creator Partnerships Strategy: WHAT I WANT YOU TO KNOW

House brand: Words to Keep. Product: a 7 x 10 guided legacy journal, $19.99 paperback, filled in by hand by a parent or grandparent for the people who will keep it. Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HKF57FVC

Written September 2026. Every follower count in this document is approximate, taken from public sources on the date noted, and will drift. Check the live profile before you send anything. Where a number could not be verified, it says so. No handle in this file was invented.

The brand has no face. Every message is signed "the Words to Keep team". The creator's honest reaction is the content. We never script a testimonial and we never ask for a positive review.

## 1. Who we want (audience-overlap map)

The buyer is usually a daughter, 35 to 60, buying for a parent 60 to 85, often after a quiet moment of realizing she does not actually know that much about her mother. The writer is the parent. The real reader is whoever holds the book in twenty years. Creators earn their place on this list by reaching one of those three people at the moment the thought "I should ask her" is already in the room.

| Cluster | Who they reach | Why the book lands there | Heat |
|---|---|---|---|

Where to spend the first ninety days: caregiving, hospice, genealogy, grief. They convert, their comment sections are already full of real stories, and their creators are used to talking about hard things without flinching.

## 2. Scoring system

Score each creator 0 to 5 on eight factors. Multiply by the weight. Maximum is 75.

| Factor | Weight | 0 looks like | 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience fit | 3 | Under 25s, no family content | Adult children of aging parents, or the parents themselves, and they already talk about it |
| Engagement rate | 2 | Under 1% on recent posts, or likes with no comments | Comments outnumber what the follower count would predict; people reply to each other |
| Content quality | 1 | Blurry, rushed, all reposts | Clear, warm, well lit, they know how to hold an object to camera |
| Comment quality (do comments contain real stories?) | 2 | Emojis and "so true" | People type paragraphs about their own mother. This is the single best predictor that our book will sell from their page. |
| Trust | 2 | Sells a different supplement every week | Rarely does brand deals; when they do, the audience thanks them |
| Brand fit | 2 | Prank, outrage, cruelty, or gloss | Honest, unhurried, comfortable with feeling without wallowing |
| Cost | 1 | Asks for four figures for one post | Will accept a book and a note, or a fair micro fee |
| Ability to make authentic content with a physical book | 2 | Talking-head only, never films objects, no family on camera | Has a parent or grandparent who appears on camera, or films their own handwriting and desk regularly |

Weighted total out of 75:
- 55 and up: reach out this week, offer paid if they want it.
- 40 to 54: gift and see what happens.
- Under 40: skip, or bookmark for a seasonal gift-guide push.

Worked example: @theformidablegenealogist (Jen), TikTok, roughly 51K followers (Sept 2026 search). Professional genealogist, posts records tips, replies to comments by video.

## 3. Tiers and what we offer each

| Tier | Size | What we offer | Honest expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano | 1K to 10K | One free book, a handwritten note, an attribution link. No fee. | Reply rates are highest here (people are flattered). Views are small. The win is content we can reshare with permission and the occasional genuine sale. Sometimes a nano creator has a 3,000-person Facebook group that outsells a 300K TikTok. |
| Micro | 10K to 100K | Free book plus, if they ask, $50 to $250 per post depending on niche. Affiliate link on top. | The core of the program. Best cost per real story. Expect 1 in 4 gifted creators to post without being chased. |
| Mid | 100K to 500K | Free book plus $250 to $1,000 per deliverable, or affiliate only if they prefer. | They will want a rate card conversation. Worth it for caregiving, hospice and genealogy names. One good mid post can move a few hundred dollars of books in a week; do not expect more. |
| Large | 500K plus | Gift with no ask, plus an open door. Paid only if the fit is unusually good and there is budget in the $2,000 scenario. | Almost never reply to cold DMs. The move is a beautiful package with a real note and zero pressure. If one of them posts unprompted, that is the year's best result. |

Three ways to work, and what each is worth:

- Free book gifting. Send the book, ask for nothing. No post is owed. Cheapest, most honest, slowest. Roughly one in four to one in five gifted micro creators will post something. Budget: a KDP author copy is roughly $5 to $8 to print (check the KDP printing cost calculator for the exact number) plus shipping.
- Paid posts. A fee for a specific deliverable (one video, one story set, one newsletter mention). Predictable timing, less predictable sincerity. Always pair with the rule that they say what they actually think. Paying for the post is fine; paying for the opinion is not.
- Affiliate. Amazon Associates (anyone with a site or channel can apply; standard commission on physical books is a few percent and changes, so check the current rate card) or the Amazon Influencer Program storefront (creators with an established social following). At $19.99 a book, a creator earns well under a dollar a copy. Nobody will work for that alone. Treat affiliate as a courtesy that lets them earn a little, not as compensation.

Disclosure, in one paragraph. Under the FTC Endorsement Guides (revised July 2023), any material connection between us and a creator must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously in the post itself, not buried in a bio or behind "more". A free book counts as a material connection, even when no post was required. So every creator we send a book to should say so in plain words: "Words to Keep sent me this book" or "#gifted" alongside a plain-language line, and "#ad" or "paid partnership" for paid work. Video disclosures should be in the video and the caption. We tell creators this in the first message so nobody is surprised. We never ask a creator to hide the relationship, never ask them to remove a disclosure, and never supply a script.

Amazon reviews. A creator who received a free book may leave an Amazon review only if it is unconditional (no post or review was required to receive the book), it is their honest opinion, and it discloses that the book was a gift from the publisher. Amazon's community guidelines prohibit reviews given in exchange for compensation and reviews from anyone with a financial interest, so paid creators should not review on Amazon at all. We never ask for a positive review, never ask for a review as a condition of anything, and never offer anything in exchange for one. If a creator asks "do you want me to review it on Amazon?", the answer is: only if you want to, only if it is honest, and only with a line saying we sent it.

## 4. Tracking

Promo codes are not available on KDP, so attribution links are the tool.

- Amazon Attribution. Free, inside the Amazon Ads console, and available to KDP authors who sell in the Amazon store. Create one attribution tag per creator, and where a creator posts on more than one platform, one per platform (for example "Jen-TikTok" and "Jen-YouTube"). The dashboard reports clicks, detail page views, add to carts, and purchases for both paperback and ebook. It cannot see sales from people who saw the video and searched Amazon later, so treat reported sales as a floor.
- Brand Referral Bonus. Amazon pays back a percentage of sales driven by off-Amazon traffic that runs through attribution tags. It requires enrollment in Amazon Brand Registry, which requires a registered or pending trademark (pending via the Amazon IP Accelerator route) and a Professional seller account. As of this writing, Words to Keep has no trademark and no Brand Registry, so the bonus is not available. Attribution still works without it. If the brand grows, registering "Words to Keep" as a trademark is the unlock; put it on the decisions list rather than assuming it.
- UTM parameters on anything off-Amazon (a landing page, a Substack, a link-in-bio). Use a consistent pattern: `utm_source=creator_handle`, `utm_medium=tiktok|instagram|youtube|substack|facebook`, `utm_campaign=gifting_2026q4` and so on. Keep the handle in the source so the tracking table and the analytics match.
- Ask every creator to use their attribution link, and put the same link in their storefront if they have one. If they insist on their own Associates link, that is fine; we lose attribution visibility on that creator and note it in the table.
- Weekly: pull Attribution numbers into the tracking table in section 9. Monthly: rescore the creators who posted, using real views and clicks.

## 6. Content ideas creators can do without being told

1. Fill in the giver's page for your own parent, on camera, and mail it that day.
2. Read one prompt out loud to your mother on FaceTime and post her answer. "What did your grandmother's house smell like?" is the one that works every time.
3. The "if you only fill out ten pages" challenge: pick the ten pages you most want your parent to answer, show them, tag a sibling to pick theirs.
4. A grandparent answers one "Quick ones" prompt straight to camera: "my coffee order", "the word I always mispronounced", "what I always said at the door".
5. "Things I never said" stitch: read one prompt from Part Seven ("Something I never said because I thought you already knew") and answer it about your own parent, then ask the comments to.
6. The recipe page: film your parent writing down the family recipe by hand, including the part that is not written anywhere.
7. "The song to play, and the one to skip": ask your parent which song should play when you miss them, and which will wreck you. Post the answer with the song.
8. Ten years later reaction: an adult who received a filled-in book (or an old letter in a parent's handwriting) reading it now. Real handwriting only.
9. Genealogist version: "I have 400 years of records and not one page in my grandmother's handwriting." Show the book as the record you can still create.
10. Sibling pool: three siblings each write a question on their "A question from your family" page before wrapping it, and film the parent reading them.

Also honest and useful: "I gave my dad this book and he has done two pages in three months." That is a real result and the book says so on the ten-pages page. Let creators post it.

## 8. Monthly cadence

Assumptions, and they are rough: cold gifting DMs to nano and micro creators get a reply from roughly 20 to 35 percent; cold paid pitches to mid creators from roughly 10 to 20 percent; anything to a large creator, low single digits. Of gifted creators who accept a book, roughly one in four to one in five posts unprompted within six weeks. These are industry-typical ranges, not measured results for this brand; replace them with real numbers after the first sixty days.

Baseline rhythm, any budget:
- Week 1: score 15 new creators, send 10 first messages.
- Week 2: send 10 more, mail books to anyone who replied.
- Week 3: send 10 more, send the single follow-up to week 1 recipients.
- Week 4: pull Attribution data, rescore, write two lines per creator in the tracker, choose next month's 30.

Thirty outreaches a month at the ranges above yields roughly 6 to 10 replies, 5 to 8 books mailed, and 1 to 3 posts. That is the honest scale of a $0 program. It compounds: posts become proof for the next round of messages, and creators who liked the book bring friends.

### $0 per month
- Cost is author copies plus postage, which is not literally zero; budget roughly $50 to $80 for 6 to 8 books mailed.
- All gifting, nano and micro only, plus one large-creator gift a month with no ask.
- Focus on genealogy and caregiving, where reply rates are best.
- Expected output: 1 to 3 creator posts a month, a slow trickle of attributed sales, a folder of content we can reshare with permission.

### $500 per month
- Same gifting base (about $70).
- Two paid micro videos a month at $100 to $200 each, in caregiving or genealogy, brief attached.
- One newsletter mention a month at $50 to $100 (Substack authors often accept this).
- Expected output: 3 to 6 posts a month, one of them predictable in timing, which matters before Mother's Day, Father's Day and November.

### $2,000 per month
- Gifting base ($100, mail more).
- Four paid micro videos ($600 total).
- One paid mid creator per month at $500 to $1,000, rotating hospice, caregiving, genealogy, gift guide.
- Reserve $200 to $300 for shipping two-copy "send it to your mom" packages and a nicer wrap for large-creator gifts.
- Expected output: 6 to 12 posts a month, one mid-tier post that can move a few hundred dollars of books in its week. Still not a growth engine on its own; it is a proof-and-content engine that feeds ads and the Amazon page.

Seasonal shape: the year's demand is in April (Mother's Day), June (Father's Day), and October through early December (holiday). Gift-guide creators close their holiday lists in October. Front-load paid spend into those windows and spend the quiet months on gifting and relationship building.

What to stop doing: if a cluster produces no posts after 30 messages, drop it for a quarter. If a paid creator's post produces fewer than 20 attributed clicks, do not rebook them, whatever the view count said.
