# Recurring Series

Series beat one-off virality. A follower should know what they are getting and want the next one.
Ten flagship series are fully developed (each can run 50 to 200 episodes from the manuscript alone).
Twenty more are sketched for rotation. Every series ends on the same sign-off frame.

Episode source rule: the manuscript has 202 prompts, 66 quick ones, 16 finish-these stems, 13
openers, and 8 letters. That is roughly 300 episode seeds before a single follower story or
photograph comes in. No series is allowed to reuse a prompt another series used in the same 30 days
(tracked in `../CONTENT_DATABASE.md`).

## The ten flagship series

### S1. Not As a Mother

**Concept.** One prompt per episode that reveals the person before the role. Named after the book's
line "Tell me about your mother, not as a mother." Also runs as "Not As a Father."
**Example episodes.** Describe her bedroom at sixteen. What did she want to be, and what happened to
that? Who was the adult who saw something in her? The first time she fell in love, not the story she
tells, what it felt like. What did she get away with?
**Hook format.** "There's a version of your mother you've never met. Here's how to meet her." Or the
prompt alone, on screen, over an old-photo texture.
**Visual format.** Cream text on ink, one line at a time; or a period-correct object (a 1978 bedroom
wall, a Walkman, a hairbrush) with the prompt over it; or a creator reading the prompt to their mom.
**CTA.** "Ask her tonight. Send this to the sibling who'll ask first."
**Why it scales.** Every prompt in Parts One and Two is an episode. Then every follower answer is a
second episode ("what you told us about your mothers").
**100 episodes.** 40 prompts from Parts One to Three, 30 follower-answer roundups (permissioned),
20 "the same question, three decades" variants (a mother at sixteen in 1965, 1980, 1995), 10 duets
with creators' real footage.

### S2. Your Parents Were People Too

**Concept.** The funny one. Evidence that your parents had a first car, a stupid haircut, a lie they
would tell again, a curfew they broke.
**Example episodes.** What's a lie you told back then that you'd tell again? The dumbest thing you did
that you survived. The skill you faked. What you drove, rode, or walked. The record you played too loud.
**Hook format.** "Your dad was 17 once." "Your mom broke curfew." "Somebody broke your grandmother's heart."
**Visual format.** Quick cuts of period objects; or a text-only "receipt" of a parent's teenage years;
or a creator asking their dad on camera and getting a four-minute answer.
**CTA.** "Ask them. Then come back and tell us."
**Why it scales.** Comedy plus nostalgia has the widest reach in the category. It recruits Segment B
(grandchildren) and men.
**100 episodes.** 30 prompts from Part Two, 20 from Part Four's funny side, 30 reader replies
("my dad's answer to the dumbest thing he survived"), 20 decade-specific versions.

### S3. The Version That Actually Happened

**Concept.** Every family has a story it tells at every gathering. This series asks for the real one.
**Example episodes.** The story your family tells at every gathering. Now the version that actually
happened. Who tells it wrong? What got left out on purpose? The joke Dad tells badly: tell it badly.
**Hook format.** "Your family has one story it tells at every gathering. Tag the person who tells it wrong."
**Visual format.** Two columns: "the story" / "what actually happened," typed in; or a slow zoom on a
holiday-table photo; or stitch bait: "tell us yours."
**CTA.** "Write down the real one. There's a page for it."
**Why it scales.** It is a game families already play. Comments write the episodes.
**100 episodes.** 10 prompt episodes, 60 follower stories (permissioned, first names only), 30
"stories that changed with every telling" formats (the fish that grew).

### S4. When You Miss Me

**Concept.** The book's Part Twelve, one page at a time: practical, specific, and often funny. The
series that will be shared most among people who have already lost someone, and the one that must
never be marketed with a clock.
**Example episodes.** The song to play, and the one to skip because it will wreck you. The thing to
cook, and what it should taste like. The dumb, specific thing that should remind you of me: not a
sunset, something with a brand name. Don't keep the house like a museum. What to do on my birthday.
My coffee order.
**Hook format.** "Not a sunset. Something with a brand name." "Don't keep the house like a museum."
"This isn't a sad section. It's a practical one."
**Visual format.** Objects: a coffee cup, a jar of a specific brand, a car radio; cream text over ink.
Voice: quiet, unhurried, a little dry.
**CTA.** "Ask them what the brand-name thing is. Write it down." Pinned: the book.
**Why it scales.** Grief content is enormous online and almost all of it is sad. This is the useful,
funny corner of it. Comments will be the best on the account.
**100 episodes.** 20 prompts from Part Twelve, 50 follower answers ("the brand-name thing that reminds
me of my dad" will run for years), 30 "the one to skip" song episodes.

### S5. Questions Nobody Asks

**Concept.** The nineteen marked prompts, and their cousins. The series that carries the brand line.
**Example episodes.** Who do you still talk to in your head? What did people in your family not talk
about? Was there ever a time you almost left, or were almost left? The risk you didn't take. What did
you hide from the people you love, to protect them? What did you do for money that never went on a résumé?
**Hook format.** "Nobody asks parents this." "Ask your dad this and watch his face."
**Visual format.** The marked-prompt ring from the book, then the prompt; minimal, slow, one line.
**CTA.** "Ask one tonight."
**Why it scales.** The marked prompts are the product's signature and each one produces different
answers from every family.
**100 episodes.** 19 marked prompts, 40 near-marked prompts, 41 reader answers and creator asks.

### S6. Ten Pages

**Concept.** The book's own list: "If you only fill out ten pages." As a challenge, a checklist, a
carousel, a weekly countdown, and a series where one creator does one page a week.
**Example episodes.** The ten list. "Which of the ten would your mom refuse?" "Ten pages: Dad edition."
"We asked ten strangers which of the ten they'd answer first."
**Hook format.** "Nobody finishes a book like this. Ten pages is a treasure."
**Visual format.** Carousel checklist; a hand ticking boxes; a ten-week creator series.
**CTA.** "Screenshot this. Send it to them."
**Why it scales.** Saves and screenshots. Honest about completion, which disarms the biggest objection.
**100 episodes.** 10 single-page deep dives, 20 relationship variants (grandma, stepdad, husband),
40 creator page-a-week episodes, 30 follower completions.

### S7. Things I Never Said

**Concept.** The finish-these stems from Part Seven, offered to the audience one at a time.
**Example episodes.** "I never told you that ______." "Thank you for ______. I don't think I ever
said it." "I was wrong about ______." "Something I misunderstood about you for a long time."
**Hook format.** The stem, alone, on screen, with a blank. No voice.
**Visual format.** Handwritten stem on a ruled page; a pen waiting; five seconds of nothing.
**CTA.** "Finish it in the comments, or finish it to them."
**Why it scales.** The blank does the work. People finish sentences compulsively.
**100 episodes.** 16 stems, then 84 "what you finished" compilations (permissioned; first names only).

### S8. Ask Your Grandparent This

**Concept.** The grandchild's series. Prompts that only a grandparent can answer, plus the era pages.
**Example episodes.** What a loaf of bread cost the year you were born. The song on every radio. The
first news event you remember. Something everyone had then that nobody has now. A tradition you
assumed every family had.
**Hook format.** "Your grandparents know stories you'll never find online."
**Visual format.** A grandchild filming a grandparent's answer (creator UGC); or era stills with the prompt.
**CTA.** "Call them. Ask one. Post the answer with #askyourgrandparent."
**Why it scales.** TikTok's core audience has living grandparents and loves them publicly. UGC-native.
**100 episodes.** 12 era prompts, 30 Part One prompts, 58 grandchild-filmed answers.

### S9. The Story Behind This Photograph

**Concept.** One real photograph (permissioned, from a follower or creator), one question the photo
raises, one answer. Never AI people. Never stock.
**Example episodes.** Who took this? What happened right after? Why is nobody smiling? Whose car?
**Hook format.** "You know this person. Do you know who they were?"
**Visual format.** Slow push-in on the photo; the question types over it; the answer in the caption.
**CTA.** "Find one photo. Ask one question. Tape it in the book."
**Why it scales.** Every family has a shoebox. Submissions never run out.
**100 episodes.** Entirely follower and creator submissions, with a template that keeps quality even.

### S10. One Question Tonight

**Concept.** The daily. A single question, posted in the evening, sized for a phone screen and a
kitchen table. The account's heartbeat.
**Example episodes.** Any prompt. Rotated across parts so no two nights feel alike.
**Hook format.** "One question tonight:" then the prompt.
**Visual format.** Static or 6-second video. Ink ground, cream type. Same frame every night.
**CTA.** "Ask it. Reply with what they said."
**Why it scales.** Consistency is the follow reason. Zero production cost. The reply thread is the
content database for every other series.
**100 episodes.** 300 available before repeating.

## Series hygiene

- Each series has a fixed opening frame and a fixed sign-off frame so viewers recognize it in 0.5 s.
- Log every episode in `../CONTENT_DATABASE.md` with its prompt so no prompt repeats within 30 days.
- Retire a series after three consecutive below-median episodes; revive it with a format change, not a wording change.
