# Hook Library

Organized by psychological structure, not by wording. A hook is the first line on screen or in the
mouth, 0 to 3 seconds. Rules: no clock, no "before it's too late," no invented claims, quote the
book where the book is better. Mark used hooks in `../CONTENT_DATABASE.md` so they are not repeated
within 30 days.

## Curiosity (an open loop the viewer has to close)

- There's a version of your mother you've never met.
- Ask your dad this and watch his face.
- Your mom had a bedroom at sixteen. Do you know what was on the walls?
- What did your parents argue about? You were there. Do you know what it was really about?
- Your dad did something for money that never went on a résumé.
- There's one question in this book that most parents skip. It's not the one you think.
- Your grandmother was somebody's first love.
- Tell me about your mother, not as a mother. (the book, page 1 of Part One)

## Surprise / contradiction

- The best question to ask your dad isn't about you.
- Don't ask your mom what she wants for Christmas. Ask her this instead.
- The saddest section of this book is the funniest.
- "What advice would you give your younger self" is the worst question you can ask a parent. Here's the one that works.
- Your parents' love story has a version they don't tell. That's the one to ask for.
- Nobody finishes a book like this. That's fine.

## Nostalgia

- Your dad had a first car. What was it?
- The record your mom played too loud.
- What did your grandmother's house smell like?
- A word your family used that no one else does.
- The song from that one summer. Your parents had one too.
- The first phone number you ever memorized. Your mom still knows hers.

## Loss aversion (without a clock)

- There's a phone number your mother still knows by heart. Do you know whose?
- Every family has a story that almost disappeared.
- Some things are too important to leave unsaid.
- The stories don't leave when they do. They leave when nobody wrote them down.
- You know your parents. You don't know all of them.

## Identity / family identity

- Your family has one story it tells at every gathering. Tag the person who tells it wrong.
- Every family has a word nobody outside the family understands.
- If your family doesn't talk about something, this is the page for it.
- Your family's version of "I love you" is probably a sentence at the door.

## Humor

- Your dad is going to say he doesn't have any stories. He's lying.
- Your dad was 17 once. Legally.
- What's the dumbest thing your father did that he survived? Ask him. Clear your afternoon.
- Not a sunset. Something with a brand name.
- Your mom broke curfew. Ask her how.
- Every dad has a worst boss story and it is at least eleven minutes long.

## Confession

- Something I never said because I thought you already knew.
- I was wrong about ______.
- Thank you for ______. I don't think I ever said it.
- Something I should have apologized for, and didn't.

## Suspense / "wait for it"

- The last section of this book has no questions. Just names and room.
- Four pages. No prompt. Most people write it last.
- She answered nine of the ten. Guess which one she skipped.
- I gave my dad a book of questions. He skipped straight to page 131.

## Emotional payoff (the hook is the reward, delivered late)

- Ask them who they still talk to in their head.
- What to do on my birthday. (a mother's answer, with permission)
- Don't keep the house like a museum.
- The one to skip, because it will wreck you.

## Unanswered question (comment bait that is a real question)

- If you had one more hour with them, what would you ask?
- What's the sentence your parent says at the door?
- What's the brand-name thing that reminds you of your dad?
- Which of these ten would your mom refuse to answer?

## Unexpected truth

- Most memory journals ask the same fifty questions. This one asks the two hundred that come after.
- Your parents are more likely to write the truth than say it.
- The reason your dad won't answer isn't the question. It's the audience. Give him a page instead.

## Visual surprise / pattern interrupt

- [A ruled page. A pen. Five seconds of nothing. Then:] Four pages. No prompt.
- [A coffee cup, a specific brand.] This is my mother.
- [Text only, black screen.] Not the title. The Tuesday.
- [An open book, upside down, in a father's hands.] He said he wouldn't. He's on page 60.

## "You never thought about this"

- Your parents had an entire life before you, and most of it is undocumented.
- Your mom has a friend whose number she still knows by heart.
- The reason your family tells the same story every year is that nobody has written down the real one.
- Your dad's first paycheck went somewhere. Ask where.

## Share bait (the impulse is "send this to Mom")

- Send this to the sibling who'll actually ask.
- Send this to your mom with no comment.
- Forward this to your dad and screenshot his reply.
- Ask your grandmother this on Sunday.

## Save bait

- The ten questions to ask if you only ask ten.
- Twelve things to ask on the drive home for the holidays.
- Six questions your grandfather can answer in one line each.

## Hooks specific to the giver (conversion)

- How to give a book like this so it actually gets written in.
- Fill in the first page before you wrap it. Say why.
- Ask one question out loud when you hand it over. The first answer is the hardest.
- Don't ask when it'll be done. Ask what they've written lately.

## Retired (a competitor owns it, or it breaks the rules)

- "Questions you'll wish you asked" (competitor's title; do not use as a hook, series, or hashtag)
- "Before it's too late" / "before they're gone" / "while you still can"
- "Time is running out" / any birthday-countdown framing
