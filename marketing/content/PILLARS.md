# Content Pillars

Eighteen pillars. Each is a standing subject the account can post about forever, with a reason to
exist, the manuscript source it draws on, and the behavior it is built to trigger. Series (repeatable
formats) live in `SERIES.md`; pillars are the subjects the series are made from.

| # | Pillar | What it is | Manuscript source | Built to trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Questions you should ask your parents** | One good question, delivered plainly, with a reason it works | The marked prompts; quick ones | Save, send to sibling |
| 2 | **Not as a mother** (your parents were people first) | The version of them before the role: sixteen, first job, first love, bedroom walls | Parts One, Two; "Tell me about your mother, not as a mother" | Curiosity, tag Mom |
| 3 | **Things you don't know about your dad** | Men, funny and specific: worst boss, dumbest thing survived, money that never went on a résumé | Parts Two, Four | Humor, tag Dad, comments |
| 4 | **Things you don't know about your mom** | The private life: what she wanted, what she was afraid of, who broke her heart | Parts One, Three | Tag Mom, shares |
| 5 | **The version that actually happened** | Family stories vs. the truth; sibling disputes; the joke told badly | Part One spread; Part Twelve "the joke to tell" | Comments, tags, duets |
| 6 | **When you miss me** | Practical, funny, specific: the song, the recipe, the brand-name thing, the house not a museum | Part Twelve | Saves, shares among the bereaved, deep comments |
| 7 | **Things I never said** | Unfinished sentences: "I never told you that," "Thank you for ______. I don't think I ever said it." | Part Seven | Confession comments, shares to a parent |
| 8 | **Old photographs** | One photo, one question: who took it, what happened right after | Photo pages; "the story behind" | Nostalgia, UGC with real photos |
| 9 | **Family sayings and the words we use** | "A word your family used that no one else does." "What we always said. What it meant." | Part One quick ones; Part Eleven family-sayings page | Comments (everyone has one), regional identity |
| 10 | **Ask your grandparent this** | Grandparent-specific: the year they were born, what a loaf of bread cost, the first news they remember | "The year I was born"; Part One | Grandchild UGC, TikTok native |
| 11 | **The last time you…** | Pattern-interrupt: the last time you were carried to bed, the last phone number you memorized | Part Three quick ones; Part Two | Nostalgia, saves |
| 12 | **If you only ask ten questions** | The book's own ten-page list as a public challenge; variants by relationship | Front matter "If you only fill out ten pages" | Saves, screenshots, challenge UGC |
| 13 | **Stories that almost disappeared** | Real, permissioned stories from followers and creators that were almost never told (no invention) | The giver's guide; the whole premise | Shares, community |
| 14 | **One night, one question** | "If you had one night: what would you ask?" and the answers people leave | Part Eleven "What I'd say if I only had a minute" | Comment volume, duet fuel |
| 15 | **Handwriting** | The object: pen on paper, crossed-out words, "write your name the way you sign it" | Closing page "In my own hand"; permission page | Visual, ASMR-adjacent, product proof |
| 16 | **Not the title, the Tuesday** | Work and money as lived: first rent, first paycheck, the job you'd do again for free | Part Four | Relatable, cross-generational comments |
| 17 | **What I believe now** | Beliefs, changed minds, being right vs. being kind | Part Eight | Quotable, older-audience shares on Facebook |
| 18 | **How to give a book like this** | The giver's craft: write why, add your own question, ask one out loud, don't ask when it'll be done | Giver's guide page; family-question pages | Directly converts; educates the buyer |

## Balance rule

In any week: at most 1 in 5 posts shows the book as the subject. The rest show the questions, the
stories, the photographs, the handwriting. The book appears in the sign-off frame, the pinned comment,
and the link. Pillars 15 and 18 are the only ones where the book is the subject on purpose.
