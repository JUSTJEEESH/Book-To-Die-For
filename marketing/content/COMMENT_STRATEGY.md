# Comment Strategy: turning comments into content, without using anyone

Comments on this account will contain people's dead parents. That is the responsibility and the
opportunity. The rule that governs everything below: **a comment about a loss is answered as a
person, never as a store.** Selling happens in the pinned comment and the link, not in replies.

## 1. Reply protocol

| Comment type | Reply in this order | Never |
|---|---|---|
| A story about a living parent ("my dad said…") | Thank them; ask one specific follow-up question ("What did he say the boss's name was?"); ask permission to feature: "Can we share this in a roundup, first name only?" | Correct their story; sell |
| A story about a parent who has died | One sentence about them, using the detail they gave ("A Folgers can. That's exactly the kind of thing the book means."); nothing else | Link, hashtag, "have you seen our book," any CTA |
| "I wish I had asked…" | "What would you have asked, if you had one more hour?" (this is the seed for C-13) | Sympathy templates; "so sorry for your loss" as a reflex |
| A question about the book | Answer plainly, with the page number if relevant; link in the pinned comment, not the reply | Overclaim; "bestseller"; "life-changing" |
| "My mom would never do this" | "Most people don't finish it. Ten pages is the goal. Which one would she do first?" | Argue |
| A joke | Match it, briefly | Explain the joke |
| Criticism (price, KDP quality, "just another journal") | Agree with what is true (it is print-on-demand; it is $17.99), state what is different in one sentence, stop | Defensiveness; deleting the comment |
| Spam or cruelty | Hide. Do not reply. | Engage |

Speed: reply within the first hour on every new post (the algorithm's window), then twice a day.

## 2. Comment-response video templates

A comment becomes a video when it (a) asks a question the book answers, (b) tells a story that
suggests a new prompt, or (c) expresses the regret the brand exists to prevent, and the commenter
has agreed.

**Template A: the question back.**
Screen: the comment (anonymized if requested). Voice or text: one question in return. Example:
"I wish I'd asked my dad more questions." → "What would you ask him if you had one more hour?"
12 seconds. No book on screen. Pinned comment carries the link.

**Template B: the page that answers it.**
Screen: the comment. Then the page in the book that matches it, photographed. Example: "Does it ask
about grief or just happy stuff?" → Part Six's opener, "You can skip this whole section. That isn't a
formality. It's the rule." 15 seconds.

**Template C: what you told us.**
A roundup of 10 to 20 real answers to one prompt, first names only, each on screen for one second.
Example: the brand-name things (C-46). Only with permission per commenter, asked in the reply thread.

**Template D: the correction.**
When a comment gets something wrong about the book (for example "it's just for grandparents"), show
the page that proves otherwise (the "how to use" line: "Where the book says mother, father, child, or
family, it means whoever those people were for you."). Never scold.

## 3. Pinned-comment strategy

Every post has one pinned comment, written before the post goes live. Three kinds:

- **The page.** "There's a page for this in the book. Link in bio." (default)
- **The ask.** "Tell us what they said. We read every one." (on prompts built for stories)
- **The permission.** "If you'd rather not ask this one, that's allowed. There's a page in the book
  that says so." (on the hard prompts)

Never pin a link under a post whose subject is loss (C-05, C-13, C-24, C-33). Pin "the ask" or
nothing.

## 4. Conversation starters (captions and first comments)

Designed to generate:

**Stories.** "What's the worst boss your dad ever had? Name optional." · "What did your grandmother's
house smell like?" · "The story your family tells every year. Who tells it wrong?"

**Shares.** "Send this to the sibling who'll actually ask." · "Send this to your mom with no comment."
· "Forward to your dad. Screenshot his reply."

**Tags.** "Tag the person who still knows the phone number." · "Tag whoever's cooking on Thursday."
· "Tag the cousin who was there."

**Saves.** "Screenshot this for the drive home." · "Save this for Sunday's call." · "The ten. Save it."

**Comments (real questions).** "Coffee order?" · "What does your family say at the door?" · "The
brand-name thing?" · "Which of the ten would she refuse?"

## 5. Comments as a research feed

Every comment that contains a specific detail (an object, a phrase, a job, a place) is logged in
`../research/COMMENT_LOG.md` with the post ID. That log is the source for roundups, for new prompts
in future editions, and for the weekly review's "strongest topics."

## 6. What we do not do

- No fabricated comments, ever. Not one. Not as an example on screen.
- No "engagement bait" that asks for a comment without a reason ("comment YES if you love your mom").
- No replying to grief with a link.
- No screenshots of comments without permission, except anonymized and paraphrased.
- No pretending the account is a person who lost a parent. The account is a publisher with a point of view.
