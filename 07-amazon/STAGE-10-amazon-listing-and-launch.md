## 2. Product description (HTML-formatted for KDP; 1,650 characters)

<b>Some things are too important to leave unsaid.</b>

You know some of their stories. You know the person, or the version they've shown you. This book is for the rest.

<b>What I Want You to Know</b> is a guided legacy journal for a parent or grandparent to fill in by hand, for the people who will keep it. It begins with the house they grew up in and ends with a letter. In between are the questions nobody thinks to ask: what they were afraid of, what they changed their mind about, what they never said, what they hope you'll forgive, and what to do on the days you miss them.

<b>Not "tell me your story." Their words, for you.</b>

Most memory journals are written from the child's point of view, as a list of questions. This one is written in the parent's voice. The title is the first thing they're saying to you. The last section is a letter.

<b>Inside:</b>
<ul>
<li>200 prompts across 13 parts, from childhood to what they believe now to what they want you to know</li>
<li>One prompt per page, with real room to write. The most important prompts get a two-page spread</li>
<li>"The questions nobody thinks to ask," marked throughout</li>
<li>A page at the end of every part where <i>you</i> write a question of your own before you give it</li>
<li>A "When You Miss Me" section: the song to play, the recipe to make, the joke to tell</li>
<li>Eight letters, including one last letter, with room to write them</li>
<li>Family tree, a decade timeline, the important people, family sayings, a recipe in their handwriting, a signature page, and an index of every prompt</li>
<li>Large, easy-to-read prompts, and a note for anyone who finds writing hard: talk, and let someone write it down</li>
</ul>

<b>For everyone in the family.</b> Written for a mother, father, grandmother, grandfather, stepparent, or anyone whose story you want to keep. The giver's page at the front makes it theirs before it's opened.

7 x 10 inches. 254 pages. Cream paper. Matte cover. Made to be written in, and kept.

<b>Write it while you're here. Keep it forever.</b>

## 3. Seven bullet points (for A+ or the "About this item" module, if used)

1. **Written in their voice, not yours.** Every other memory journal asks "tell me your story." This one lets a parent or grandparent speak directly to the people who will read it.
2. **The questions nobody thinks to ask.** What they were afraid of. What they changed their mind about. What they never said. What they hope you'll forgive.
3. **Room to actually write.** 7 x 10 inches, one prompt per page, two-page spreads for the ones that matter. No cramped boxes.
4. **Your questions, too.** A page at the end of every part where the giver writes a question of their own before wrapping it.
5. **A section for the days you miss them.** The song, the place, the recipe, the joke, and the things they don't want you to do.
6. **Ends with letters.** To each child, to a partner, to grandchildren, to the family, and one last letter with four full pages.
7. **One book for every parent and grandparent.** Nothing inside assumes who they are to you. Buy one for each of them.

Supporting claims available for A+ and ads, all true of the built book: large 21-point prompts, a two-page index of every prompt, a one-page guide for the giver, a dictate-or-record option for anyone who finds writing hard, and a decade timeline.

## 13. Mother's Day angle (the largest window: orders peak 10–3 days before)

Message: *You've asked her everything except the things that matter.*
Creative: the Part Seven opener, and the prompt "Something I never said because I thought you already knew."
Timing: ads live 5 weeks out; email and social 3 weeks out; price back to list 3 days before.
Bundle idea: two copies, one for Mom and one for Grandma, in a single social post ("one for each of them").

## 14. Father's Day angle

Message: *He'll say he doesn't have any stories. Give him this anyway.*
Creative: "The worst boss you ever had," "What did you do for money that never went on a résumé?" and "What's the dumbest thing you did that you survived?" Men respond to the funny and the specific; lead with those, and let the last letter be the surprise.
Timing: 4 weeks out. Fathers are bought for later than mothers.

## 15. Christmas angle

Message: *The one gift that's still there in fifty years.*
Creative: the book under a tree is a cliché; instead, the giver's page being filled in at a kitchen table. "This book was given to Grandpa by all of us."
Timing: ads from November 1; the hardcover is the Christmas format; watch KDP print lead times and message "order by" dates.
Family angle: grandchildren pooling for a grandparent. Use the "A question from your family" page: every grandchild writes one.

## 16. Evergreen angle

Message: *Their story deserves to outlive them.*
Triggers that never stop: a 70th birthday, retirement, a diagnosis, a move to a smaller home, a new grandchild, a funeral that made someone realize what they never asked. Evergreen ads target "gift for parents" and "memory book" phrases at a steady low bid, and the product-targeting campaign on competitor ASINs runs all year.

## 18. What would make it number one

Honest arithmetic. A narrow sub-category badge in this space typically requires tens of sales per day
for a few hours. The main Journal Writing category needs hundreds per day in season. Reviews are the
multiplier: at 100 reviews with a 4.7 average and the thumbnail contrast, paid traffic converts at a
rate that makes the top of a sub-category reachable in the Mother's Day window. The plan above is
built to get to 100 reviews by week 12. If it does, aim the first serious ad budget at Mother's Day.
