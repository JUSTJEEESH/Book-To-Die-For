@@ page letter-opening
This book is yours.

Someone gave it to you because they want more of you than they have. Not the version they already know. The rest.

There are no rules here. Skip anything. Write two words on a page, or run into the margins. Answer a question with a completely different story, because that's the one that showed up. Tape in a photograph. Draw. Write in pencil and change your mind.

This is not homework. Nobody is grading it. Nobody is even waiting, not really. They're hoping.

Some of these questions will be easy. Some will surprise you. A few will ask for things you've never said out loud. Those are the ones most worth answering, and the ones you are most allowed to leave blank.

Write it in your own hand if you can. Your handwriting is part of what they're keeping.

If you bought this book for yourself, without anyone asking, then you already know why. Somebody is going to want this someday. You're just getting there first.

Start anywhere. Start today if you can.

@@ page howto
How to use this book

Any order. Any pace. Any pen. A fine ballpoint or fine-tip pen shows through least.

One question to a page. The important ones get two.

A small mark beside a question means: nobody usually asks this one.

Each part ends with a page of quick ones, and a page with a question from your family. That question was written by the person who gave you this book, before they wrapped it.

Where the book says mother, father, child, or family, it means whoever those people were for you.

Blank pages are for photographs, drawings, recipes, or anything the book didn't think to ask for.

You decide who reads this, and when.

@@ page ten
If you only fill out ten pages

Nobody finishes every page of a book like this. If you only do ten, do these.

- Tell me about your mother, not as a mother.
- The story your family tells at every gathering. Now the version that actually happened.
- The first time you fell in love.
- Tell me about a kind of love you didn't expect.
- What got you through?
- Something I never said because I thought you already knew.
- The rule you live by that you've never said out loud.
- What I want you to know about me that has nothing to do with being your parent.
- A tradition to start after I'm gone.
- One last letter.

@@ page permission
You can skip anything.

Any question. Any page. Any part. Nothing in this book is owed. What you leave blank says nothing about you, and what you fill in is yours to fill in any way you like.

@@ opener PART ONE | Where I Came From
Start small. The house. The street. The kitchen at six in the morning. Nobody is asking for a history. They're asking what it was like to be you, before anyone called you by the name they call you now.

Some of this you've told before. Tell it again, the long way. Some of it you've never said out loud. That's the part they'll read twice.
